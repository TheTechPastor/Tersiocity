## **The core difference**

### **Daily Diary**

**“What happened today?”**

Concrete, chronological, factual.
### **Journal**

**“What does today mean?”**

Reflective, interpretive, thematic.

They intentionally live side-by-side on the **Today** page, but serve _different cognitive roles_.

---

## **Daily Diary (main Today page)**

**Purpose:** capture the day _as it unfolds_

Typical use:

- Events
- Appointments
- Tasks completed
- Who you met
- What you did
- Brief notes, timestamps
    
Characteristics:

- Short
- Linear
- Often bullet-based
- Low friction
- Minimal reflection

Think:

> _Logbook / ship’s log_

> “Departed harbor at 0900. Wind NW. Met John. Fixed router.”

  

This is especially useful for:

- Memory anchoring
- Accountability
- Later recall (“when did I…?”)
- Cross-referencing projects or people

---

## **Journal (Time Garden Journal section)**

**Purpose:** process the day _after or during it_

Typical use:

- Reflections
- Emotions
- Insights
- Prayers
- Lessons learned
- Meaning-making
- Discernment
    
Characteristics:

- Paragraphs
- Slower writing
- Thematic rather than chronological
- Often written once/day (or less)
    
Think:

> _Captain’s reflection_

> “Today reminded me how easily I overcommit. I felt peace once I slowed down…”

This is where:

- Growth happens
- Patterns emerge
- Faith, values, and identity are explored
    

---

## **Why Time Garden separates them**


This separation avoids two common PKM failures:

### **1.** ### **Over-thinking daily notes**


If everything is “journal,” people stop writing.

Daily Diary stays _easy_.

  ### **2.** **Losing reflection in noise**

If everything is “log,” insight gets buried.

Journal stays _intentional_.

Time Garden is quietly enforcing a **good spiritual & cognitive practice**:


> _Record first. Reflect second._
