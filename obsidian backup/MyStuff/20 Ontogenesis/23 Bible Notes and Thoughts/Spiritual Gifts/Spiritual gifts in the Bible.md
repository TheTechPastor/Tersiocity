Here is a summary of the key spiritual gifts found in the Bible and their uses:

## Spiritual Gifts in the Bible

### 1 Corinthians 12:4-11
- **Wisdom**: The ability to apply knowledge and make wise decisions[1][3][4]
- **Knowledge**: The ability to understand and teach biblical truths[1][3][4] 
- **Faith**: Extraordinary trust and confidence in God[1][3][4]
- **Healing**: The ability to supernaturally cure illnesses and injuries[1][3][4]
- **Miraculous Powers**: The ability to perform miracles[1][3][4]
- **Prophecy**: The ability to speak God's message and reveal the future[1][3][4]
- **Distinguishing between Spirits**: The ability to discern if a message is from God, Satan, or a person's own thoughts[1][3][4]
- **Tongues**: The ability to speak in unknown languages[1][3][4] 
- **Interpretation of Tongues**: The ability to translate tongues for the church[1][3][4]

### Romans 12:6-8
- **Prophecy**: The ability to speak God's message[1][4][5]
- **Serving**: The ability to help meet the practical needs of others[1][3][4]  
- **Teaching**: The ability to instruct others in biblical truth[1][3][4]
- **Encouraging**: The ability to motivate and strengthen others[1][3][4]
- **Giving**: The ability to generously share resources[1][3][4]
- **Leadership**: The ability to guide and direct others[1][3][4] 
- **Mercy**: The ability to empathize and care for those in need[1][3][4]

### Ephesians 4:11
- **Apostles**: Those sent out to establish new churches[3][4][5]
- **Prophets**: Those who speak God's messages[1][3][4]
- **Evangelists**: Those who effectively share the gospel[3][4][5]
- **Pastors**: Those who shepherd and care for a local church[3][4][5]
- **Teachers**: Those who instruct others in biblical truth[1][3][4]

## Views on Spiritual Gifts

There are differing views on spiritual gifts among Christians:

1. **Continuationist View**: Believes all the spiritual gifts mentioned in the Bible are still active and available to believers today. This view sees the gifts as essential for the ongoing work of the church.

2. **Cessationist View**: Believes certain miraculous gifts like tongues, prophecy, and healing were only temporary, meant to authenticate the apostles and the early church. These gifts are no longer necessary or active today.

3. **Modified Continuationist View**: Believes most spiritual gifts are still active, but the most miraculous gifts may have ceased or are less common. The focus is on using the gifts that build up the church.

Regardless of one's view, the Bible is clear that spiritual gifts are given by the Holy Spirit to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ. The purpose is to glorify God and edify the church, not to elevate individuals[1][3][4][5].
