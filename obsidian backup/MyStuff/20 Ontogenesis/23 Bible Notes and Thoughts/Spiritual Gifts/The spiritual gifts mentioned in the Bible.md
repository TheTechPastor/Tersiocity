The spiritual gifts mentioned in the Bible are spread across several key passages, mainly in the New Testament. These gifts are provided by the Holy Spirit to believers for the purpose of building up the body of Christ—the Church. The primary passages that discuss these gifts include Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12-14, Ephesians 4, and 1 Peter 4. Below is a list of the spiritual gifts mentioned in these passages, along with their descriptions, uses, characteristics, and the biblical references where each is found.

### Romans 12:6-8

- **Prophecy**: The proclamation of a message from God, often involving divine insight into current situations or future events. It is used for edification, exhortation, and comfort of the believers (1 Corinthians 14:3).
- **Serving/Ministry**: The gift of serving is the ability to identify and meet the practical needs of others in the church, often in behind-the-scenes roles.
- **Teaching**: The ability to understand and communicate biblical truth in a clear and relevant manner so that others will learn.
- **Exhortation**: The gift of exhortation involves encouraging, comforting, and admonishing others so that they grow in their faith.
- **Giving**: This gift involves the ability to generously contribute material resources and financial support to the work of the church and the needs of others, often beyond what is expected or common.
- **Leadership**: The ability to influence, motivate, and provide direction to God's people in a manner that brings about spiritual growth and accomplishing various church missions.
- **Mercy**: The capacity to feel deep empathy for those in need and to provide practical help and comfort to alleviate their suffering.

### 1 Corinthians 12:4-11, 28-30

- **Wisdom**: The ability to apply spiritual truths effectively to specific situations in the church or individuals' lives.
- **Knowledge**: The gift of knowledge is a deep understanding of the Scripture or certain aspects of God's will, which is used to guide and advise others.
- **Faith**: Exceptional confidence in God's promises, power, and presence, enabling the believer to trust God in extraordinary ways.
- **Healing**: The supernatural ability to restore health without medical means, often instantaneously.
- **Miraculous Powers**: Performing signs and wonders that give evidence of the presence and power of God.
- **Distinguishing between Spirits**: The ability to discern the source of spiritual manifestations, whether they originate from God, human emotion, or demonic forces.
- **Tongues**: The ability to speak in a language unknown to the speaker, often as a sign for unbelievers (1 Corinthians 14:22) and for personal edification (1 Corinthians 14:4).
- **Interpretation of Tongues**: The ability to understand and convey the meaning of an utterance spoken in tongues.

### Ephesians 4:11

- **Apostleship**: The foundation-laying role that involves leadership, church planting, and cross-cultural missions, often accompanied by signs and wonders.
- **Evangelism**: The ability to effectively communicate the gospel to non-believers in a way that leads them to accept Christ as their savior.
- **Pastor/Shepherd**: The gift of shepherding involves leading, feeding, nurturing, comforting, and protecting a community of believers, often in a local church context.

### 1 Peter 4:9-11

- **Hospitality**: The ability to welcome and care for those in need of food, lodging, and fellowship, often strangers or those without a community.

### Various Views on Spiritual Gifts

- **Cessationism**: This view holds that certain miraculous gifts (e.g., prophecy, tongues, healing) ceased with the apostolic age and the completion of the New Testament canon.
- **Continuationism**: The belief that all the spiritual gifts have continued to the present day and are available for Christians, although they may manifest differently.
- **Charismatic/Pentecostal**: These traditions emphasize the ongoing availability and importance of all spiritual gifts, including the more overtly supernatural ones, for the life and mission of the church.
- **Sacramental Views**: In traditions such as Catholicism and Orthodoxy, spiritual gifts are often understood within the broader context of the sacraments and the church's liturgical life, with a strong emphasis on the unity and edification of the church.

Each of these views shapes how individuals and communities understand and practice the use of spiritual gifts, influencing everything from worship services to ministry activities.
