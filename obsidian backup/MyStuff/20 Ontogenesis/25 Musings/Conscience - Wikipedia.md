Moral philosophy or values of an individual

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_022.jpg/220px-Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_022.jpg)

[Vincent van Gogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_van_Gogh "Vincent van Gogh"), 1890. [Kröller-Müller Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%B6ller-M%C3%BCller_Museum "Kröller-Müller Museum"). *The Good Samaritan* (after [Delacroix](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Good_Samaritan_\(Delacroix_1849\).jpg)).

A **conscience** is a [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") process that elicits [emotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotion "Emotion") and rational associations based on an individual's [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics "Ethics") or value system. Conscience stands in contrast to elicited emotion or thought due to associations based on immediate sensory perceptions and reflexive responses, as in sympathetic [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") responses. In common terms, conscience is often described as leading to feelings of [remorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remorse "Remorse") when a person commits an act that conflicts with their [moral values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_value "Moral value"). The extent to which conscience informs moral judgment before an action and whether such [moral judgments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_judgment "Moral judgment") are or should be based on [reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason "Reason") has occasioned debate through much of modern history between theories of basics in ethic of human life in juxtaposition to the theories of [romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism "Romanticism") and other reactionary movements after the end of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages").

Religious views of conscience usually see it as linked to a morality inherent in all humans, to a beneficent universe and/or to [divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity "Divinity"). The diverse ritualistic, mythical, doctrinal, legal, institutional and material features of religion may not necessarily cohere with experiential, emotive, [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") or [contemplative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplative "Contemplative") considerations about the origin and operation of conscience.[^1] Common [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular "Secular") or [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific "Scientific") views regard the capacity for conscience as probably [genetically determined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics"), with its subject probably learned or [imprinted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprinting_\(psychology\) "Imprinting (psychology)") as part of a [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture "Culture").[^2]

Commonly used metaphors for conscience include the "voice within", the "inner light",[^autogenerated2000-3] or even Socrates' reliance on what the Greeks called his "[daimōnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemon_\(classical_mythology\)#Socrates "Daemon (classical mythology)") sign", an averting (ἀποτρεπτικός *apotreptikos*) inner voice heard only when he was about to make a mistake. Conscience, as is detailed in sections below, is a concept in national and international law,[^autogenerated1948-4] is increasingly conceived of as applying to the world as a whole,[^autogenerated2001-5] has motivated numerous notable acts for the public good[^artforamnesty1-6] and been the subject of many prominent examples of literature, music and film.[^7]

Although humanity has no generally accepted definition of conscience or universal agreement about its role in [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics "Ethics") decision-making, three approaches have addressed it:[^autogenerated176-8]

1. [Religious views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion")
2. [Secular views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism "Secularism")
3. [Philosophical views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy")

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/SeatedBuddhaGandhara2ndCenturyOstasiatischeMuseum.jpg/170px-SeatedBuddhaGandhara2ndCenturyOstasiatischeMuseum.jpg)

Seated [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha "Buddha"), [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara "Gandhara"), 2nd century CE. The Buddha linked conscience with compassion for those who must endure cravings and suffering in the world until right conduct culminates in right mindfulness and right contemplation.

In the literary traditions of the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads"), [Brahma Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Sutras "Brahma Sutras") and the [Bhagavad Gita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad_Gita "Bhagavad Gita"), conscience is the label given to attributes composing knowledge about good and evil, that a [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_\(spirit\) "Soul (spirit)") acquires from the completion of acts and consequent accretion of [karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma "Karma") over many lifetimes.[^9] According to [Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara "Adi Shankara") in his *[Vivekachudamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivekachudamani "Vivekachudamani")* morally right action (characterised as humbly and compassionately performing the primary duty of good to others without expectation of material or spiritual reward), helps "purify the heart" and provide mental tranquility but it alone does not give us "direct perception of the Reality".[^10] This knowledge requires discrimination between the eternal and non-eternal and eventually a realization in [contemplation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplation "Contemplation") that the true self merges in a universe of pure consciousness.[^11]

In the [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian "Zoroastrian") faith, after death a soul must face judgment at the *Bridge of the Separator*; there, [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil "Evil") people are tormented by prior denial of their own higher nature, or conscience, and "to all time will they be guests for the *House of the Lie*."[^12] The [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") concept of [Ren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren_\(Confucianism\) "Ren (Confucianism)"), indicates that conscience, along with social etiquette and correct relationships, assist humans to follow *The Way* ([Tao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao "Tao")) a mode of life reflecting the implicit human capacity for goodness and harmony.[^13]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Bronze_Marcus_Aurelius_Louvre.jpg/170px-Bronze_Marcus_Aurelius_Louvre.jpg)

[Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius "Marcus Aurelius") bronze fragment, Louvre, Paris: "To move from one unselfish action to another with God in mind. Only there, delight and stillness."

Conscience also features prominently in [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism").[^14] In the [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali") scriptures, for example, [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha "Buddha") links the positive aspect of *conscience* to a pure heart and a calm, well-directed mind. It is regarded as a spiritual power, and one of the "Guardians of the World". The Buddha also associated conscience with compassion for those who must endure cravings and suffering in the world until right conduct culminates in right mindfulness and right [contemplation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplation "Contemplation").[^15] [Santideva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santideva "Santideva") (685–763 CE) wrote in the [Bodhicaryavatara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhicaryavatara "Bodhicaryavatara") (which he composed and delivered in the great northern Indian Buddhist university of [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda "Nalanda")) of the spiritual importance of perfecting virtues such as [generosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generosity "Generosity"), [forbearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbearance "Forbearance") and training the awareness to be like a "block of wood" when attracted by vices such as [pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride "Pride") or [lust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lust "Lust"); so one can continue advancing towards right understanding in meditative absorption.[^16] *Conscience* thus manifests in Buddhism as unselfish love for all living beings which gradually intensifies and awakens to a purer awareness[^17] where the mind withdraws from sensory interests and becomes aware of itself as a single whole.[^18]

The [Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Emperor "Roman Emperor") [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius "Marcus Aurelius") wrote in his *[Meditations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditations "Meditations")* that conscience was the human capacity to live by rational principles that were congruent with the true, tranquil and harmonious nature of our mind and thereby that of the Universe: "To move from one unselfish action to another with God in mind. Only there, delight and stillness ... the only rewards of our existence here are an unstained character and unselfish acts."[^19]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Munqidh_min_al-dalal_%28last_page%29.jpg/170px-Munqidh_min_al-dalal_%28last_page%29.jpg)

Last page of [Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali "Al-Ghazali")'s autobiography in MS Istanbul, Shehid Ali Pasha 1712, dated [A.H.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Hegirae "Anno Hegirae") 509 = 1115–1116. Ghazali's crisis of epistemological skepticism was resolved by "a light which God Most High cast into my breast ... the key to most knowledge."

The [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic "Islamic") concept of *[Taqwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqwa "Taqwa")* is closely related to conscience. In the [Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān") verses 2:197 & 22:37 Taqwa refers to "right conduct" or "[piety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piety "Piety")", "guarding of oneself" or "guarding against evil".[^20] [Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān") verse 47:17 says that God is the ultimate source of the believer's taqwā which is not simply the product of individual will but requires inspiration from God.[^21] In [Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān") verses 91:7–8, God the Almighty talks about how He has perfected the soul, the conscience and has taught it the wrong (fujūr) and right (taqwā). Hence, the awareness of vice and virtue is inherent in the soul, allowing it to be tested fairly in the life of this world and tried, held accountable on the day of judgment for responsibilities to God and all humans.[^22]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Sura49.pdf/page1-170px-Sura49.pdf.jpg)

[Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān") Sura 49. Surah al-Hujurat, 49:13 declares: "come to know each other, the noblest of you, in the sight of God, are the ones possessing taqwá".

[Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān") verse 49:13 states: "O humankind! We have created you out of male and female and constituted you into different groups and societies, so that you may come to know each other-the noblest of you, in the sight of God, are the ones possessing taqwā." In [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), according to eminent theologians such as [Al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali "Al-Ghazali"), although events are ordained (and written by God in al-Lawh al-Mahfūz, the *Preserved Tablet*), humans possess free will to choose between wrong and right, and are thus responsible for their actions; the conscience being a dynamic personal connection to God enhanced by knowledge and practise of the [Five Pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam "Five Pillars of Islam"), deeds of piety, repentance, self-discipline and prayer; and disintegrated and metaphorically covered in blackness through sinful acts.[^23] [Marshall Hodgson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Hodgson "Marshall Hodgson") wrote the three-volume work: *The Venture of Islam: Conscience and History in a World Civilization*.[^24]

In the Protestant Christian tradition, [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther "Martin Luther") insisted in the [Diet of Worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Worms "Diet of Worms") that his conscience was captive to the Word of God, and it was neither safe nor right to go against conscience. To Luther, conscience falls within the ethical, rather than the religious, sphere.[^tillich-25] [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin "John Calvin") saw conscience as a battleground: "the enemies who rise up in our conscience against his Kingdom and hinder his decrees prove that God's throne is not firmly established therein".[^26] Many [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") regard following one's conscience as important as, or even more important than, obeying human [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority "Authority").[^27] According to the bible, written in Romans 2:15, conscience is the one bearing witness, accusing or excusing one another, so we would know when we break the law written in our hearts; the guilt we feel when we do something wrong tells us that we need to repent."[^28] This can sometimes (as with the conflict between [William Tyndale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tyndale "William Tyndale") and [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More "Thomas More") over the translation of the Bible into English) lead to moral quandaries: "Do I unreservedly obey my Church/priest/military/political leader or do I follow my own inner feeling of right and wrong as instructed by prayer and a personal reading of scripture?"[^29] Some contemporary Christian churches and religious groups hold the moral teachings of the [Ten Commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Commandments "Ten Commandments") or of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") as the highest authority in any situation, regardless of the extent to which it involves responsibilities in law.[^30] In the [Gospel of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_John "Gospel of John") (7:53–8:11) (King James Version) Jesus challenges those accusing a woman of adultery stating: "'He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.' And again he stooped down, and wrote on the ground. And they which heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one" \[However the word 'conscience' is not in the original New Testament Greek, and is not in the vast majority of Bible versions.\] (see [Jesus and the woman taken in adultery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_and_the_woman_taken_in_adultery "Jesus and the woman taken in adultery")). In the [Gospel of Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke "Gospel of Luke") (10: 25–37) Jesus tells the story of how a despised and heretical [Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan "Samaritan") (see [Parable of the Good Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parable_of_the_Good_Samaritan "Parable of the Good Samaritan")) who (out of compassion/pity - the word 'conscience' is not used) helps an injured stranger beside a road, qualifies better for eternal life by loving his neighbor, than a priest who passes by on the other side.[^31]

[Nikiforos Lytras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikiforos_Lytras "Nikiforos Lytras"), *Antigone in front of the dead Polynices* (1865), oil on canvas, National Gallery of Greece-Alexandros Soutzos Museum

This dilemma of obedience in conscience to divine or state law, was demonstrated dramatically in [Antigone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigone "Antigone")'s defiance of [King Creon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creon_of_Thebes "Creon of Thebes")'s order against burying her brother an alleged traitor, appealing to the "[unwritten law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law")" and to a "longer allegiance to the dead than to the living".[^32]

[Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology") sees conscience as the last practical "judgment of reason which at the appropriate moment enjoins \[a person\] to do good and to avoid evil".[^33] The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council "Second Vatican Council") (1962–65) describes: "Deep within his conscience man discovers a law which he has not laid upon himself but which he must obey. Its voice, ever calling him to love and to do what is good and to avoid evil, tells him inwardly at the right movement: do this, shun that. For man has in his heart a law inscribed by God. His dignity lies in observing this law, and by it he will be judged. His conscience is man’s most secret core, and his sanctuary. There he is alone with God whose voice echoes in his depths."[^34] Thus, conscience is not like the will, nor a habit like prudence, but "the interior space in which we can listen to and hear the truth, the good, the voice of God. It is the inner place of our relationship with Him, who speaks to our heart and helps us to discern, to understand the path we ought to take, and once the decision is made, to move forward, to remain faithful"[^35] In terms of logic, conscience can be viewed as the practical conclusion of a moral syllogism whose major premise is an objective norm and whose minor premise is a particular case or situation to which the norm is applied. Thus, Catholics are taught to carefully educate themselves as to revealed norms and norms derived therefrom, so as to form a correct conscience. Catholics are also to examine their conscience daily and with special care before [confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confession_\(religion\) "Confession (religion)"). Catholic teaching holds that, "Man has the right to act according to his conscience and in freedom so as personally to make moral decisions. He must not be forced to act contrary to his conscience. Nor must he be prevented from acting according to his conscience, especially in religious matters".[^36] This right of conscience does not allow one to arbitrarily disagree with Church teaching and claim that one is acting in accordance with conscience. A sincere conscience presumes one is diligently seeking moral truth from authentic sources, that is, seeking to conform oneself to that moral truth by listening to the authority established by Christ to teach it. Nevertheless, despite one's best effort, "\[i\]t can happen that moral conscience remains in ignorance and makes erroneous judgments about acts to be performed or already committed ... This ignorance can often be imputed to personal responsibility ... In such cases, the person is culpable for the [wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil "Evil") he commits."[^37] Thus, if one realizes one may have made a mistaken judgment, one's conscience is said to be vincibly erroneous and it is not a valid norm for action. One must first remove the source of error and do one's best to achieve a correct judgment. If, however, one is not aware of one's error or if, despite an honest and diligent effort one cannot remove the error by study or seeking advice, then one's conscience may be said to be invincibly erroneous. It binds since one has subjective certainty that one is correct. The act resulting from acting on the invincibly erroneous conscience is not good in itself, yet this deformed act or material sin against God's right order and the objective norm is not imputed to the person. The formal obedience given to such a judgment of conscience is good. Some Catholics appeal to conscience in order to justify dissent, not on the level of conscience properly understood, but on the level of the principles and norms which are supposed to inform conscience. For example, some priests make on the use of the so-called [internal forum solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_and_external_forum_\(Catholic_canon_law\)#%22Internal_forum_solution%22 "Internal and external forum (Catholic canon law)") (which is not sanctioned by the [Magisterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magisterium "Magisterium")) to justify actions or lifestyles incompatible with Church teaching, such as Christ's prohibition of remarriage after divorce or sexual activity outside marriage.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact">[<i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>]</sup> The [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") has warned that "rejection of the Church's authority and her teaching ... can be at the source of errors in judgment in [moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral "Moral") conduct".[^38] An example of someone following his conscience to the point of accepting the consequence of being condemned to death is Sir [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More "Thomas More") (1478-1535).[^39] A theologian who wrote on the distinction between the 'sense of duty' and the 'moral sense', as two aspects of conscience, and who saw the former as some feeling that can only be explained by a divine Lawgiver, was [John Henry Cardinal Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Cardinal_Newman "John Henry Cardinal Newman").[^40] A well known saying of him is that he would first toast on his conscience and only then on the pope, since his conscience brought him to acknowledge the authority of the pope.[^41]

conscience.[^autogenerated2000-3] [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry "Freemasonry") describes itself as providing an adjunct to religion and key symbols found in a "circumscribe their desires and keep their passions within due bounds toward all mankind."[^spoilt-45] The historian [Manning Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manning_Clark "Manning Clark") viewed Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy "Leo Tolstoy"), after a decade studying the issue (1877–1887), held that the only power capable of resisting the evil associated with materialism and the drive for social power of religious institutions, was the capacity of humans to reach an individual spiritual truth through reason and conscience.[^47] Many prominent [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious "Religious") works about conscience also have a significant philosophical component: examples are the works of [Al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali "Al-Ghazali"),[^autogenerated1966-48] [Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna "Avicenna"),[^autogenerated67-49] [Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquinas "Aquinas"),[^autogenerated145-50] [Joseph Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Butler "Joseph Butler")[^autogenerated365-51] and [Dietrich [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") thought that any animal endowed with well-marked social instincts would inevitably acquire a moral sense or conscience, as its intellectual powers approximated man's.

The secular approach to conscience includes [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"), [physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), [sociological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology"), [humanitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarianism "Humanitarianism"), and [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") views.[^autogenerated1983-53] [Lawrence Kohlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Kohlberg "Lawrence Kohlberg") considered *critical conscience* to be an important psychological stage in the proper moral development of humans, associated with the capacity to rationally weigh principles of responsibility, being best encouraged in the very young by linkage with humorous personifications (such as [Jiminy Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiminy_Cricket "Jiminy Cricket")) and later in adolescents by debates about individually pertinent moral dilemmas.[^54] [Erik Erikson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Erikson "Erik Erikson") placed the development of *conscience* in the 'pre-schooler' phase of his eight stages of normal human personality development.[^55] The psychologist [Martha Stout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Stout "Martha Stout") terms *conscience* "an intervening sense of obligation based in our emotional attachments."[^56] Thus a good conscience is associated with feelings of integrity, psychological wholeness and peacefulness and is often described using adjectives such as "quiet", "clear" and "easy".[^57]

[Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud "Sigmund Freud") regarded *conscience* as originating psychologically from the growth of [civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilisation "Civilisation"), which periodically frustrated the external expression of [aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggression "Aggression"): this destructive impulse being forced to seek an alternative, healthy outlet, directed its energy as a [superego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superego "Superego") against the person's own "ego" or selfishness (often taking its cue in this regard from parents during childhood).[^58] According to Freud, the consequence of not obeying our conscience is [guilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilt_\(emotion\) "Guilt (emotion)"), which can be a factor in the development of [neurosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurosis "Neurosis"); Freud claimed that both the cultural and individual super-ego set up strict ideal demands with regard to the moral aspects of certain decisions, disobedience to which provokes a 'fear of conscience'.[^59]

[Antonio Damasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Damasio "Antonio Damasio") considers conscience an aspect of [extended consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_consciousness "Extended consciousness") beyond survival-related dispositions and incorporating the search for truth and desire to build norms and ideals for behavior. [^60]

#### Conscience as a society-forming instinct

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Jeremy_Bentham_by_Henry_William_Pickersgill.jpg/170px-Jeremy_Bentham_by_Henry_William_Pickersgill.jpg)

[Jeremy Bentham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham "Jeremy Bentham"): "[Fanaticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanaticism "Fanaticism") never sleeps ... it is never stopped by *conscience*; for it has pressed *conscience* into its service."

[Michel Glautier](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Michel_Glautier&action=edit&redlink=1 "Michel Glautier (page does not exist)") argues that conscience is one of the [instincts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instincts "Instincts") and drives which enable people to form societies: groups of humans without these drives or in whom they are insufficient cannot form societies and do not reproduce their kind as successfully as those that do.[^61]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/WP_Eichmann_Passport.jpg/170px-WP_Eichmann_Passport.jpg)

War criminal [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann "Adolf Eichmann") in passport used to enter Argentina: his conscience spoke with the "respectable voice" of the indoctrinated wartime German society that surrounded him.

[Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") considered that *conscience* evolved in humans to resolve conflicts between competing natural impulses-some about self-preservation but others about safety of a family or community; the claim of conscience to [moral authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_authority "Moral authority") emerged from the "greater duration of impression of social instincts" in the struggle for survival.[^62] In such a view, behavior destructive to a person's [society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society") (either to its structures or to the persons it comprises) is bad or "evil".[^63] Thus, conscience can be viewed as an outcome of those biological drives that prompt humans to avoid provoking [fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear "Fear") or [contempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt "Contempt") in others; being experienced as [guilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilt_\(emotion\) "Guilt (emotion)") and [shame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shame "Shame") in differing ways from society to society and person to person.[^64] A requirement of conscience in this view is the capacity to see ourselves from the point of view of another person.[^65] Persons unable to do this ([psychopaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathy "Psychopathy"), [sociopaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathy#Psychopathy_vs._sociopathy "Psychopathy"), [narcissists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissistic_personality_disorder "Narcissistic personality disorder")) therefore often act in ways which are "evil".[^66]

Fundamental in this view of conscience is that humans consider some "other" as being *in a social relationship*. Thus, [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") is invoked in conscience to quell [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal "Tribal") conflict and the notion of a Brotherhood of Man is invoked to quell [national conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War "War"). Yet such crowd drives may not only overwhelm but redefine individual *conscience*. [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche "Friedrich Nietzsche") stated: "communal solidarity is annihilated by the highest and strongest drives that, when they break out passionately, whip the individual far past the average low level of the 'herd-conscience.'"[^67] [Jeremy Bentham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham "Jeremy Bentham") noted that: "[fanaticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanaticism "Fanaticism") never sleeps ... it is never stopped by *conscience*; for it has pressed *conscience* into its service."[^68] [Hannah Arendt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Arendt "Hannah Arendt") in her study of the trial of [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann "Adolf Eichmann") in Jerusalem, notes that the accused, as with almost all his fellow Germans, had lost track of his *conscience* to the point where they hardly remembered it; this wasn't caused by familiarity with atrocities or by psychologically redirecting any resultant natural pity to themselves for having to bear such an unpleasant duty, so much as by the fact that anyone whose *conscience* did develop doubts could see no one who shared them: "Eichmann did not need to close his ears to the voice of conscience ... not because he had none, but because his conscience spoke with a "respectable voice", with the voice of the respectable society around him".[^69]

[Sir Arthur Keith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Arthur_Keith "Sir Arthur Keith") in 1948 developed the [Amity-enmity complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amity-enmity_complex "Amity-enmity complex"). We evolved as tribal groups surrounded by enemies; thus conscience evolved a dual role; the duty to save and protect members of the [in-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group "In-group"), and the duty to show hatred and aggression towards any [out-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingroups_and_outgroups "Ingroups and outgroups").

An interesting area of research in this context concerns the similarities between our relationships and those of [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), whether animals in human society ([pets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pets "Pets"), [working animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_animal "Working animal"), even animals grown for food) or in the wild.[^70] One idea is that as people or animals perceive a social relationship as important to preserve, their *conscience* begins to respect that former "other", and urge actions that protect it.[^71][^72] Similarly, in complex territorial and cooperative breeding [bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird "Bird") communities (such as the [Australian magpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_magpie "Australian magpie")) that have a high degree of etiquettes, rules, hierarchies, play, songs and negotiations, rule-breaking seems tolerated on occasions not obviously related to survival of the individual or group; behaviour often appearing to exhibit a touching gentleness and tenderness.[^73]

#### Evolutionary biology

Contemporary scientists in [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology") seek to explain conscience as a function of the [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain "Brain") that evolved to facilitate [altruism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altruism "Altruism") within societies.[^74] In his book *[The God Delusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_God_Delusion "The God Delusion")*, [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") states that he agrees with [Robert Hinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hinde "Robert Hinde")'s *Why Good is Good*, [Michael Shermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Shermer "Michael Shermer")'s *The Science of Good and Evil*, [Robert Buckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Buckman "Robert Buckman")'s *Can We Be Good Without God?* and [Marc Hauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Hauser "Marc Hauser")'s *Moral Minds*, that our sense of right and wrong can be derived from our [Darwinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinian "Darwinian") past. He subsequently reinforced this idea through the lens of the [gene-centered view of evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene-centered_view_of_evolution "Gene-centered view of evolution"), since the unit of natural selection is neither an individual organism nor a group, but rather [the "selfish" gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Selfish_Gene "The Selfish Gene"), and these genes could ensure their own "selfish" survival by, *inter alia*, pushing individuals to act altruistically towards its kin.[^75]

#### Neuroscience and artificial conscience

Numerous case studies of [brain damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_damage "Brain damage") have shown that damage to areas of the [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain "Brain") (such as the anterior [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex")) results in the reduction or elimination of [inhibitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inhibition "Social inhibition"), with a corresponding radical change in behaviour.[^76] When the damage occurs to adults, they may still be able to perform moral reasoning; but when it occurs to children, they may never develop that ability.[^77][^78]

Attempts have been made by neuroscientists to locate the [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will") necessary for what is termed the 'veto' of conscience over unconscious mental processes (see [Neuroscience of free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience_of_free_will "Neuroscience of free will") and [Benjamin Libet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Libet "Benjamin Libet")) in a scientifically measurable awareness of an intention to carry out an act occurring 350–400 microseconds after the electrical discharge known as the 'readiness potential.'[^79][^80][^81]

Jacques Pitrat claims that some kind of artificial conscience is beneficial in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") systems to improve their long-term performance and direct their [introspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introspection "Introspection") processing.[^82]

The word "conscience" derives etymologically from the Latin *conscientia*, meaning "privity of knowledge"[^83] or "with-knowledge". The [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") word implies internal awareness of a moral standard in the mind concerning the quality of one's motives, as well as a consciousness of our own actions.[^84] Thus *conscience* considered philosophically may be first, and perhaps most commonly, a largely unexamined "gut feeling" or "vague sense of guilt" about what ought to be or should have been done.[^85] Conscience in this sense is not necessarily the product of a process of rational consideration of the moral features of a situation (or the applicable [normative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normative "Normative") principles, rules or laws) and can arise from parental, peer group, religious, state or corporate [indoctrination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoctrination "Indoctrination"), which may or may not be presently consciously acceptable to the person ("traditional conscience").[^86] *Conscience* may be defined as the [practical reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_reason "Practical reason") employed when applying moral convictions to a situation ("critical conscience").[^87] In purportedly morally mature mystical people who have developed this capacity through daily [contemplation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplation "Contemplation") or [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation "Meditation") combined with selfless service to others, *critical conscience* can be aided by a "spark" of intuitive insight or revelation (called [marifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marifa "Marifa") in [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic "Islamic") [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi "Sufi") philosophy and [synderesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synderesis "Synderesis") in medieval Christian [scholastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism "Scholasticism") [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_philosophy "Moral philosophy")).[^88][^langston34-89] *Conscience* is accompanied in each case by an internal awareness of 'inner light' and [approbation](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/approbation "wikt:approbation") or 'inner darkness' and condemnation as well as a resulting conviction of right or duty either followed or declined.[^90]

The medieval [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic "Islamic") scholar and mystic [Al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali "Al-Ghazali") divided the concept of *[Nafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafs "Nafs")* ([soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_\(spirit\) "Soul (spirit)") or [self (spirituality)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self_\(spirituality\) "Self (spirituality)")) into three categories[^autogenerated1966-48] based on the [Qur’an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99an "Qur’an"):

1. Nafs Ammarah (12:53) which "exhorts one to freely indulge in gratifying passions and instigates to do evil"
2. Nafs Lawammah (75:2) which is "the conscience that directs man towards right or wrong"
3. Nafs Mutmainnah (89:27) which is "a self that reaches the ultimate peace"

The medieval Persian philosopher and physician [Muhammad ibn Zakariya al-Razi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Zakariya_al-Razi "Muhammad ibn Zakariya al-Razi") believed in a close relationship between *conscience* or spiritual integrity and physical health; rather than being self-indulgent, man should pursue knowledge, use his intellect and apply justice in his life.[^91] The medieval Islamic philosopher [Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna "Avicenna"), whilst imprisoned in the castle of Fardajan near [Hamadhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadhan "Hamadhan"), wrote his famous isolated-but-awake "Floating Man" [sensory deprivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_deprivation "Sensory deprivation") [thought experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought_experiment "Thought experiment") to explore the ideas of human [self-awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-awareness "Self-awareness") and the substantiality of the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_\(spirit\) "Soul (spirit)"); his hypothesis being that it is through [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence"), particularly the [active intellect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_intellect "Active intellect"), that God communicates [truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth "Truth") to the human [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind "Mind") or conscience.[^autogenerated67-49] According to the Islamic [Sufis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufis "Sufis") conscience allows [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah "Allah") to guide people to the [marifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marifa "Marifa"), the peace or "light upon light" experienced where a Muslim's prayers lead to a melting away of the self in the inner knowledge of God; this foreshadowing the eternal Paradise depicted in the [Qur’ān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur%E2%80%99%C4%81n "Qur’ān").[^92]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Jan_Van_Ruysbroeck.jpg/170px-Jan_Van_Ruysbroeck.jpg)

The Flemish mystic [Jan van Ruysbroeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Ruysbroek_\(scholar\) "Jan van Ruysbroek (scholar)") viewed a pure conscience as facilitating "an outflowing losing of oneself in the abyss of that eternal object which is the highest and chief blessedness".

Some medieval Christian [scholastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism "Scholasticism") such as [Bonaventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaventure "Bonaventure") made a distinction between conscience as a rational faculty of the mind ([practical reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_reason "Practical reason")) and inner awareness, an intuitive "spark" to do good, called *[synderesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synderesis "Synderesis")* arising from a remnant appreciation of absolute good and when consciously denied (for example to perform an evil act), becoming a source of inner torment.[^langston34-89] Early modern theologians such as [William Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Perkins_\(Puritan\) "William Perkins (Puritan)") and [William Ames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ames "William Ames") developed a syllogistic understanding of the conscience, where God's law made the first term, the act to be judged the second and the action of the conscience (as a rational faculty) produced the judgement. By debating test cases applying such understanding conscience was trained and refined (i.e. [casuistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casuistry "Casuistry")).[^93]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Avicenna.jpg/170px-Avicenna.jpg)

The medieval Persian philosopher [Ibn Sina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Sina "Ibn Sina") ([Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna "Avicenna")) developed a sensory deprivation thought experiment to explore the relationship between conscience and God.

In the 13th century, [St. Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquinas "Aquinas") regarded *conscience* as the application of moral knowledge to a particular case (S.T. I, q. 79, a. 13). Thus, conscience was considered an act or judgment of practical reason that began with [synderesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synderesis "Synderesis"), the structured development of our innate remnant awareness of absolute good (which he categorised as involving the five primary precepts proposed in his theory of [Natural Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Law "Natural Law")) into an acquired habit of applying moral principles.[^autogenerated145-50] According to Singer, Aquinas held that conscience, or *conscientia* was an imperfect process of judgment applied to activity because knowledge of the [natural law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law") (and all acts of natural virtue implicit therein) was obscured in most people by education and custom that promoted selfishness rather than fellow-feeling (*Summa Theologiae*, I–II, I).[^thomas_aquinas_pp._247-249-94] Aquinas also discussed conscience in relation to the virtue of [prudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudence "Prudence") to explain why some people appear to be less "morally enlightened" than others, their weak will being incapable of adequately balancing their own needs with those of others.[^95]

Aquinas reasoned that acting contrary to conscience is an [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil "Evil") action but an errant conscience is only blameworthy if it is the result of culpable or [vincible ignorance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincible_ignorance "Vincible ignorance") of factors that one has a duty to have knowledge of.[^thomas_aquinas_pp._247-249-94] Aquinas also argued that conscience should be educated to act towards real goods (from [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God "God")) which encouraged [human flourishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eudaimonia "Eudaimonia"), rather than the apparent goods of sensory pleasures.[^thomas_aquinas_pp._247-249-94] In his *Commentary* on [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle")'s [Nicomachean Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics "Nicomachean Ethics") Aquinas claimed it was weak will that allowed a non-virtuous man to choose a principle allowing pleasure ahead of one requiring moral constraint.[^96]

[Thomas A Kempis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A_Kempis "Thomas A Kempis") in the medieval [contemplative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplative "Contemplative") classic *[The Imitation of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Imitation_of_Christ "The Imitation of Christ")* (ca 1418) stated that the glory of a good man is the witness of a good conscience. "Preserve a quiet conscience and you will always have joy. A quiet conscience can endure much, and remains joyful in all trouble, but an evil conscience is always fearful and uneasy."[^97] The anonymous medieval author of the Christian [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystical "Mystical") work *[The Cloud of Unknowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cloud_of_Unknowing "The Cloud of Unknowing")* similarly expressed the view that in profound and prolonged [contemplation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplation "Contemplation") a soul dries up the "root and ground" of the [sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin "Sin") that is always there, even after one's [confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confession_\(religion\) "Confession (religion)") and however busy one is in [holy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy "Holy") things: "therefore, whoever would work at becoming a [contemplative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemplative "Contemplative") must first cleanse his \[or her\] conscience."[^98] The medieval Flemish mystic [John of Ruysbroeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Ruysbroeck "John of Ruysbroeck") likewise held that true conscience has four aspects that are necessary to render a man just in the active and contemplative life: "a free spirit, attracting itself through love"; "an intellect enlightened by grace", "a delight yielding propension or inclination" and "an outflowing losing of oneself in the abyss of ... that eternal object which is the highest and chief blessedness ... those lofty amongst men, are absorbed in it, and immersed in a certain boundless thing."[^99]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Arthur_Schopenhauer_Portrait_by_Ludwig_Sigismund_Ruhl_1815.jpeg/170px-Arthur_Schopenhauer_Portrait_by_Ludwig_Sigismund_Ruhl_1815.jpeg)

[Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schopenhauer "Arthur Schopenhauer") considered that the good conscience we experience after an unselfish act verifies that our true self exists outside our physical person.

[Benedict de Spinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Spinoza "Baruch Spinoza") in his [*Ethics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_\(Spinoza\) "Ethics (Spinoza)"), published after his death in 1677, argued that most people, even those that consider themselves to exercise [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will"), make moral decisions on the basis of imperfect sensory information, inadequate understanding of their mind and will, as well as emotions which are both outcomes of their contingent physical existence and forms of thought defective from being chiefly impelled by self-preservation.[^100] The solution, according to Spinoza, was to gradually increase the capacity of our reason to change the forms of thought produced by emotions and to fall in love with viewing problems requiring moral decision from the perspective of eternity.[^101] Thus, living a life of peaceful conscience means to Spinoza that reason is used to generate adequate ideas where the mind increasingly sees the world and its conflicts, our desires and passions *sub specie aeternitatis*, that is without reference to time.[^102] [Hegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegel "Hegel")'s obscure and [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystical "Mystical") [Philosophy of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_Mind "Philosophy of Mind") held that the absolute right of *freedom of conscience* facilitates human understanding of an all-embracing unity, an absolute which was rational, real and true.[^103] Nevertheless, Hegel thought that a functioning State would always be tempted not to recognize conscience in its form of subjective knowledge, just as similar non-objective opinions are generally rejected in science.[^104] A similar idealist notion was expressed in the writings of [Joseph Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Butler "Joseph Butler") who argued that conscience is [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God "God")\-given, should always be obeyed, is intuitive, and should be considered the "constitutional monarch" and the "universal moral faculty": "conscience does not only offer itself to show us the way we should walk in, but it likewise carries its own authority with it."[^105] Butler advanced ethical speculation by referring to a duality of regulative principles in human nature: first, "self-love" (seeking individual happiness) and second, "benevolence" (compassion and seeking good for another) in *conscience* (also linked to the [agape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agape "Agape") of [situational ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_ethics "Situational ethics")).[^autogenerated365-51] Conscience tended to be more authoritative in questions of moral judgment, thought Butler, because it was more likely to be clear and certain (whereas calculations of self-interest tended to probable and changing conclusions).[^106] [John Selden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Selden "John Selden") in his *Table Talk* expressed the view that an awake but excessively scrupulous or ill-trained *conscience* could hinder resolve and practical action; it being "like a horse that is not well wayed, he starts at every bird that flies out of the hedge".[^107]

As the sacred texts of ancient [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist "Buddhist") philosophy became available in German translations in the 18th and 19th centuries, they influenced philosophers such as [Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schopenhauer "Schopenhauer") to hold that in a healthy mind only deeds oppress our *conscience*, not wishes and thoughts; "for it is only our deeds that hold us up to the mirror of our will"; the *good conscience*, thought Schopenhauer, we experience after every disinterested deed arises from direct recognition of our own inner being in the phenomenon of another, it affords us the verification "that our true self exists not only in our own person, this particular manifestation, but in everything that lives. By this the heart feels itself enlarged, as by egotism it is contracted."[^108]

[Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant"), a central figure of the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment"), likewise claimed that two things filled his mind with ever new and increasing admiration and awe, the oftener and more steadily they were reflected on: "the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me ... the latter begins from my invisible self, my personality, and exhibits me in a world which has true infinity but which I recognise myself as existing in a universal and necessary (and not only, as in the first case, contingent) connection."[^109] The 'universal connection' referred to here is Kant's [categorical imperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_imperative "Categorical imperative"): "act only according to that maxim by which you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."[^110] Kant considered *critical conscience* to be an internal court in which our thoughts accuse or excuse one another; he acknowledged that morally mature people do often describe contentment or peace in the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_\(spirit\) "Soul (spirit)") after following conscience to perform a duty, but argued that for such acts to produce virtue their primary motivation should simply be duty, not expectation of any such bliss.[^111] Rousseau expressed a similar view that conscience somehow connected man to a greater [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics") unity. [John Plamenatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Plamenatz "John Plamenatz") in his critical examination of [Rousseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rousseau "Rousseau")'s work considered that *conscience* was there defined as the feeling that urges us, in spite of contrary passions, towards two harmonies: the one within our minds and between our passions, and the other within society and between its members; "the weakest can appeal to it in the strongest, and the appeal, though often unsuccessful, is always disturbing. However, corrupted by power or wealth we may be, either as possessors of them or as victims, there is something in us serving to remind us that this corruption is against nature."[^112]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/JohnLocke.png/170px-JohnLocke.png)

[John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke") viewed the widespread social fact of conscience as a justification for natural rights.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/AdamSmith1790b.jpg/170px-AdamSmith1790b.jpg)

[Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith "Adam Smith"): conscience shows what relates to ourselves in its proper shape and dimensions

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Samuel_Johnson_by_John_Opie.jpg/170px-Samuel_Johnson_by_John_Opie.jpg)

[Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Johnson "Samuel Johnson") (1775) stated that "No man's conscience can tell him the right of another man."

Other philosophers expressed a more sceptical and pragmatic view of the operation of "conscience" in society.[^113] [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke") in his *Essays on the Law of Nature* argued that the widespread fact of human conscience allowed a philosopher to infer the necessary existence of objective moral laws that occasionally might contradict those of the state.[^114] Locke highlighted the [metaethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaethics "Metaethics") problem of whether accepting a statement like "follow your *conscience*" supports [subjectivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject_\(philosophy\) "Subject (philosophy)") or [objectivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectivity_\(philosophy\) "Objectivity (philosophy)") conceptions of conscience as a guide in concrete morality, or as a spontaneous revelation of eternal and immutable principles to the individual: "if conscience be a proof of innate principles, contraries may be innate principles; since some men with the same bent of conscience prosecute what others avoid."[^115] [Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes "Thomas Hobbes") likewise pragmatically noted that opinions formed on the basis of *conscience* with full and honest conviction, nevertheless should always be accepted with humility as potentially erroneous and not necessarily indicating absolute knowledge or truth.[^116] [William Godwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Godwin "William Godwin") expressed the view that *conscience* was a memorable consequence of the "perception by men of every creed when the descend into the scene of busy life" that they possess [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will").[^117] [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith "Adam Smith") considered that it was only by developing a *critical conscience* that we can ever see what relates to ourselves in its proper shape and dimensions; or that we can ever make any proper comparison between our own interests and those of other people.[^118] [John Stuart Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill "John Stuart Mill") believed that idealism about the role of *conscience* in government should be tempered with a practical realisation that few men in society are capable of directing their minds or purposes towards distant or unobvious interests, of disinterested regard for others, and especially for what comes after them, for the idea of posterity, of their country, or of humanity, whether grounded on sympathy or on a conscientious feeling.[^mill193-194-119] Mill held that certain amount of *conscience*, and of disinterested public spirit, may fairly be calculated on in the citizens of any community ripe for [representative government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_government "Representative government"), but that "it would be ridiculous to expect such a degree of it, combined with such intellectual discernment, as would be proof against any plausible fallacy tending to make that which was for their class interest appear the dictate of justice and of the general good."[^mill193-194-119]

[Josiah Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Royce "Josiah Royce") (1855–1916) built on the [transcendental idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_idealism "Transcendental idealism") view of conscience, viewing it as the ideal of life which constitutes our moral personality, our plan of being ourself, of making common sense ethical decisions. But, he thought, this was only true insofar as our *conscience* also required loyalty to "a mysterious higher or deeper self".[^120] In the modern Christian tradition this approach achieved expression with [Dietrich Bonhoeffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietrich_Bonhoeffer "Dietrich Bonhoeffer") who stated during his imprisonment by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis "Nazis") in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") that *conscience* for him was more than practical reason, indeed it came from a "depth which lies beyond a man's own will and his own reason and it makes itself heard as the call of human existence to unity with itself."[^121] For Bonhoeffer a *guilty conscience* arose as an indictment of the loss of this unity and as a warning against the loss of one's self; primarily, he thought, it is directed not towards a particular kind of doing but towards a particular mode of being. It protests against a doing which imperils the unity of this being with itself.[^autogenerated24-52] *Conscience* for Bonhoeffer did not, like shame, embrace or pass judgment on the morality of the whole of its owner's life; it reacted only to certain definite actions: "it recalls what is long past and represents this disunion as something which is already accomplished and irreparable".[^bonhoffer66-122] The man with a *conscience*, he believed, fights a lonely battle against the "overwhelming forces of inescapable situations" which demand moral decisions despite the likelihood of adverse consequences.[^bonhoffer66-122] [Simon Soloveychik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Soloveychik "Simon Soloveychik") has similarly claimed that the *truth* distributed in the world, as the statement about human [dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity "Dignity"), as the affirmation of the line between [good and evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_and_evil "Good and evil"), lives in people as conscience.[^123]

As [Hannah Arendt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Arendt "Hannah Arendt") pointed out, however, (following the utilitarian [John Stuart Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill "John Stuart Mill") on this point): a bad conscience does not necessarily signify a bad character; in fact only those who affirm a commitment to applying moral standards will be troubled with remorse, guilt or shame by a bad *conscience* and their need to regain integrity and wholeness of the self.[^124][^125] Representing our soul or true self by analogy as our house, Arendt wrote that "conscience is the anticipation of the fellow who awaits you if and when you come home."[^autogenerated191-126] Arendt believed that people who are unfamiliar with the process of silent critical reflection about what they say and do will not mind contradicting themselves by an immoral act or crime, since they can "count on its being forgotten the next moment;" bad people are not full of regrets.[^autogenerated191-126] Arendt also wrote eloquently on the problem of languages distinguishing the word [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") from *conscience*. One reason, she held, was that *conscience*, as we understand it in moral or legal matters, is supposedly always present within us, just like *consciousness*: "and this conscience is also supposed to tell us what to do and what to repent; before it became the *lumen naturale* or [Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant")'s practical reason, it was the voice of God."[^127]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Albert_Einstein_Head_Cleaned_N_Cropped.jpg/170px-Albert_Einstein_Head_Cleaned_N_Cropped.jpg)

[Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") associated conscience with suprapersonal thoughts, feelings and aspirations.

[Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"), as a self-professed adherent of [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism") and [rationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism "Rationalism"), likewise viewed an enlightened religious person as one whose *conscience* reflects that he "has, to the best of his ability, liberated himself from the fetters of his selfish desires and is preoccupied with thoughts, feelings and aspirations to which he clings because of their super-personal value."[^128] Einstein often referred to the "inner voice" as a source of both moral and physical knowledge: "[Quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") is very impressive. But an inner voice tells me that it is not the real thing. The theory produces a good deal but hardly brings one closer to the secrets of the Old One. I am at all events convinced that He does not play dice."[^129]

[Simone Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Weil "Simone Weil") who fought for the French resistance (the [Maquis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquis_\(World_War_II\) "Maquis (World War II)")) argued in her final book *[The Need for Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Need_for_Roots "The Need for Roots"): Prelude to a Declaration of Duties Towards Mankind* that for society to become more just and protective of liberty, obligations should take precedence over rights in moral and political philosophy and a spiritual awakening should occur in the *conscience* of most citizens, so that social obligations are viewed as fundamentally having a transcendent origin and a beneficent impact on human character when fulfilled.[^130][^131] [Simone Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Weil "Simone Weil") also in that work provided a psychological explanation for the mental peace associated with a *good conscience*: "the liberty of men of goodwill, though limited in the sphere of action, is complete in that of conscience. For, having incorporated the rules into their own being, the prohibited possibilities no longer present themselves to the mind, and have not to be rejected."[^132]

Alternatives to such [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics") and [idealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism "Idealism") opinions about conscience arose from [realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism "Philosophical realism") and [materialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialism "Materialism") perspectives such as those of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"). Darwin suggested that "any [animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal") whatever, endowed with well-marked social instincts, the parental and filial affections being here included, would inevitably acquire a moral sense or conscience, as soon as its intellectual powers had become as well, or as nearly as well developed, as in man."[^133] [Émile Durkheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Durkheim "Émile Durkheim") held that the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_\(spirit\) "Soul (spirit)") and conscience were particular forms of an impersonal principle diffused in the relevant group and communicated by [totemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totemic "Totemic") ceremonies.[^134] [A. J. Ayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Ayer "A. J. Ayer") was a more recent realist who held that the existence of *conscience* was an empirical question to be answered by sociological research into the moral habits of a given person or group of people, and what causes them to have precisely those habits and feelings. Such an inquiry, he believed, fell wholly within the scope of the existing [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences "Social sciences").[^135] [George Edward Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Edward_Moore "George Edward Moore") bridged the idealistic and sociological views of 'critical' and 'traditional' conscience in stating that the idea of abstract 'rightness' and the various degrees of the specific emotion excited by it are what constitute, for many persons, the specifically 'moral sentiment' or *conscience*. For others, however, an action seems to be properly termed 'internally right', merely because they have previously regarded it as right, the idea of 'rightness' being present in some way to his or her mind, but not necessarily among his or her deliberately constructed motives.[^136]

The French philosopher [Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir "Simone de Beauvoir") in *A Very Easy Death* (*Une mort très douce*, 1964) reflects within her own *conscience* about her mother's attempts to develop such a moral sympathy and understanding of others.[^137]

> "The sight of her tears grieved me; but I soon realised that she was weeping over her failure, without caring about what was happening inside me ... We might still have come to an understanding if, instead of asking everybody to pray for my soul, she had given me a little confidence and sympathy. I know now what prevented her from doing so: she had too much to pay back, too many wounds to salve, to put herself in another's place. In actual doing she made every sacrifice, but her feelings did not take her out of herself. Besides, how could she have tried to understand me since she avoided looking into her own heart? As for discovering an attitude that would not have set us apart, nothing in her life had ever prepared her for such a thing: the unexpected sent her into a panic, because she had been taught never to think, act or feel except in a ready-made framework."

— Simone de Beauvoir. *A Very Easy Death*. Penguin Books. London. 1982. p. 60.

[Michael Walzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Walzer "Michael Walzer") claimed that the growth of religious toleration in Western nations arose amongst other things, from the general recognition that private conscience signified some inner divine presence regardless of the religious faith professed and from the general respectability, piety, self-limitation, and sectarian discipline which marked most of the men who claimed the rights of conscience.[^138] Walzer also argued that attempts by courts to define conscience as a merely personal moral code or as sincere belief, risked encouraging an anarchy of moral egotisms, unless such a code and motive was necessarily tempered with shared moral knowledge: derived either from the connection of the individual to a universal spiritual order, or from the common principles and mutual engagements of unselfish people.[^139] [Ronald Dworkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Dworkin "Ronald Dworkin") maintains that constitutional protection of [freedom of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_conscience "Freedom of conscience") is central to democracy but creates personal duties to live up to it: "Freedom of conscience presupposes a personal responsibility of reflection, and it loses much of its meaning when that responsibility is ignored. A good life need not be an especially reflective one; most of the best lives are just lived rather than studied. But there are moments that cry out for self-assertion, when a passive bowing to fate or a mechanical decision out of deference or convenience is treachery, because it forfeits dignity for ease."[^140] [Edward Conze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Conze "Edward Conze") stated it is important for individual and collective moral growth that we recognise the illusion of our conscience being wholly located in our body; indeed both our conscience and wisdom expand when we act in an unselfish way and conversely "repressed compassion results in an unconscious sense of guilt."[^141]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Peter_Singer_-_Effective_Altruism_-Melb_Australia_Aug_2015.jpg/220px-Peter_Singer_-_Effective_Altruism_-Melb_Australia_Aug_2015.jpg)

[Peter Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Singer "Peter Singer"): distinguished between immature "traditional" and highly reasoned "critical" conscience

The philosopher [Peter Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Singer "Peter Singer") considers that usually when we describe an action as conscientious in the critical sense we do so in order to deny either that the relevant agent was motivated by selfish desires, like greed or ambition, or that he acted on whim or impulse.[^142]

Moral anti-realists debate whether the moral facts necessary to activate conscience [supervene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervenience "Supervenience") on natural facts with *[a posteriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_evidence "Empirical evidence")* necessity; or arise *a priori* because moral facts have a primary intension and naturally identical worlds may be presumed morally identical.[^143] It has also been argued that there is a measure of [moral luck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_luck "Moral luck") in how circumstances create the obstacles which *conscience* must overcome to apply moral principles or human rights and that with the benefit of enforceable property rights and the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law"), access to [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care "Universal health care") plus the absence of high adult and [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") from conditions such as [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria"), [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS") and [famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine "Famine"), people in relatively prosperous developed countries have been spared pangs of *conscience* associated with the physical necessity to steal scraps of food, bribe tax inspectors or police officers, and commit murder in [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla "Guerrilla") wars against corrupt government forces or rebel armies.[^144] [Roger Scruton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Scruton "Roger Scruton") has claimed that true understanding of *conscience* and its relationship with *morality* has been hampered by an "impetuous" belief that philosophical questions are solved through the analysis of language in an area where clarity threatens vested interests.[^145] [Susan Sontag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sontag "Susan Sontag") similarly argued that it was a symptom of [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological "Psychological") immaturity not to recognise that many morally immature people willingly experience a form of delight, in some an erotic breaking of [taboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taboo "Taboo"), when witnessing violence, suffering and pain being inflicted on others.[^146] [Jonathan Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Glover "Jonathan Glover") wrote that most of us "do not spend our lives on endless landscape gardening of our self" and our *conscience* is likely shaped not so much by heroic struggles, as by choice of partner, friends and job, as well as where we choose to live.[^147] [Garrett Hardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_Hardin "Garrett Hardin"), in a famous article called "[The Tragedy of the Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tragedy_of_the_Commons "The Tragedy of the Commons")", argues that any instance in which society appeals to an individual exploiting a commons to restrain himself or herself for the general good—by means of his or her *conscience*—merely sets up a system which, by selectively diverting societal power and physical resources to those lacking in *conscience*, while fostering guilt (including anxiety about his or her individual contribution to over-population) in people acting upon it, actually works toward the elimination of conscience from the race.[^hardin68-148][^149]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/John_Ralston_Saul.jpg/170px-John_Ralston_Saul.jpg)

[John Ralston Saul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ralston_Saul "John Ralston Saul"): consumers risk turning over their conscience to technical experts and to the ideology of free markets

[John Ralston Saul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ralston_Saul "John Ralston Saul") expressed the view in *The Unconscious Civilization* that in contemporary developed nations many people have acquiesced in turning over their sense of right and wrong, their *critical conscience*, to technical experts; willingly restricting their moral freedom of choice to limited consumer actions ruled by the ideology of the free market, while citizen participation in public affairs is limited to the isolated act of voting and private-interest lobbying turns even elected representatives against the public interest.[^150]

Some argue on religious or philosophical grounds that it is blameworthy to act against *conscience*, even if the judgement of *conscience* is likely to be erroneous (say because it is inadequately informed about the facts, or prevailing moral (humanist or religious), professional ethical, legal and human rights norms).[^151] Failure to acknowledge and accept that conscientious judgements can be seriously mistaken, may only promote situations where one's conscience is manipulated by others to provide unwarranted justifications for non-virtuous and selfish acts; indeed, insofar as it is appealed to as glorifying ideological content, and an associated extreme level of devotion, without adequate constraint of external, altruistic, normative justification, **conscience** may be considered morally blind and dangerous both to the individual concerned and humanity as a whole.[^152] Langston argues that philosophers of [virtue ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtue_ethics "Virtue ethics") have unnecessarily neglected *conscience* for, once conscience is trained so that the principles and rules it applies are those one would want all others to live by, its practise cultivates and sustains the virtues; indeed, amongst people in what each society considers to be the highest state of moral development there is little disagreement about how to act.[^autogenerated176-8] [Emmanuel Levinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Levinas "Emmanuel Levinas") viewed conscience as a revelatory encountering of resistance to our selfish powers, developing morality by calling into question our naive sense of [freedom of will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_will "Freedom of will") to use such powers arbitrarily, or with [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence "Violence"), this process being more severe the more rigorously the goal of our self was to obtain control.[^levinas-153] In other words, the welcoming of the *Other*, to Levinas, was the very essence of *conscience* properly conceived; it encouraged our ego to accept the fallibility of assuming things about other people, that selfish [freedom of will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_will "Freedom of will") "does not have the last word" and that realising this has a transcendent purpose: "I am not alone ... in conscience I have an experience that is not commensurate with any a priori \[see [a priori and a posteriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_priori_and_a_posteriori "A priori and a posteriori")\] framework-a conceptless experience."[^levinas-153]

## Conscientious acts and the law

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Lester_Ott.jpg/170px-Lester_Ott.jpg)

[Lester Ott](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lester_Ott&action=edit&redlink=1 "Lester Ott (page does not exist)"), [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector "Conscientious objector") during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War")

In the late 13th and early 14th centuries, English litigants began to petition the [Lord Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chancellor "Lord Chancellor") of England for relief from unjust judgments.[^watt_page_5-154] As [Keeper of the King's Conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeper_of_the_King%27s_Conscience "Keeper of the King's Conscience"), the Chancellor intervened to allow for "merciful exceptions" to the King's laws, "to ensure that the King's conscience was right before God".[^watt_page_5-154] The Chancellor's office evolved into the [Court of Chancery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Chancery "Court of Chancery") and the Chancellor's decisions evolved into the body of law known as [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(law\) "Equity (law)").[^watt_page_5-154]

English humanist lawyers in the 16th and 17th centuries interpreted conscience as a collection of universal principles given to man by god at creation to be applied by reason; this gradually reforming the medieval [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law "Roman law")\-based system with forms of action, written pleadings, use of juries and patterns of litigation such as [Demurrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demurrer "Demurrer") and [Assumpsit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumpsit "Assumpsit") that displayed an increased concern for elements of right and wrong on the actual facts.[^155] A [conscience vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_vote "Conscience vote") in a [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament "Parliament") allows legislators to vote without restrictions from any political party to which they may belong.[^156] In his trial in Jerusalem [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") [war criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime") [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann "Adolf Eichmann") claimed he was simply following legal orders under paragraph 48 of the German Military Code which provided: "punishability of an action or omission is not excused on the ground that the person considered his behaviour required by his conscience or the prescripts of his religion".[^157] The [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") [Universal Declaration on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_on_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration on Human Rights") (UDHR) which is part of [international customary law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_customary_law "International customary law") specifically refers to *conscience* in Articles 1 and 18.[^autogenerated1948-4] Likewise, the United Nations [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights "International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights") (ICCPR) mentions *conscience* in Article 18.1.[^158]

> All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. They are endowed with reason and conscience and should act towards one another in a spirit of brotherhood

> Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion; this right includes freedom to change his religion or belief, and freedom, either alone or in community with others and in public or private, to manifest his religion or belief in teaching, practice, worship and observance

> Everyone shall have the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion. This right shall include freedom to have or to adopt a religion or belief of his choice, and freedom, either individually or in community with others and in public or private, to manifest his religion or belief in worship, observance, practice and teaching

It has been argued that these articles provide international legal obligations protecting [conscientious objectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objectors "Conscientious objectors") from service in the military.[^159]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Minnesota_anti-war_protesters_in_Washington_DC.jpg/220px-Minnesota_anti-war_protesters_in_Washington_DC.jpg)

[Nonviolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent "Nonviolent") protestors in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") in 2010 opposed to the [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War")

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Faroe_stamp_131_amnesty_international.jpg/170px-Faroe_stamp_131_amnesty_international.jpg)

Amnesty International protects prisoners of conscience. Stamp from Faroe Islands, 1986.

[John Rawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rawls "John Rawls") in his *[A Theory of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Theory_of_Justice "A Theory of Justice")* defines a [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector "Conscientious objector") as an individual prepared to undertake, in public (and often despite widespread condemnation), an action of [civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience "Civil disobedience") to a legal rule justifying it (also in public) by reference to contrary foundational social virtues (such as justice as liberty or fairness) and the principles of morality and law derived from them.[^john_rawls_1971._pp._368%e2%80%9370-160] Rawls considered civil disobedience should be viewed as an appeal, warning or admonishment (showing general respect and fidelity to the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law") by the non-violence and transparency of methods adopted) that a law breaches a community's fundamental virtue of justice.[^john_rawls_1971._pp._368%e2%80%9370-160] Objections to Rawls' theory include first, its inability to accommodate conscientious objections to the society's basic appreciation of [justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice "Justice") or to emerging moral or ethical principles (such as respect for the rights of the [natural environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment")) which are not yet part of it and second, the difficulty of predictably and consistently determining that a majority decision is just or unjust.[^161] *Conscientious objection* (also called conscientious refusal or evasion) to obeying a law, should not arise from unreasoning, naive "traditional conscience", for to do so merely encourages infantile abdication of responsibility to calibrate the law against moral or human rights norms and disrespect for democratic institutions.[^162] Instead it should be based on "critical conscience' – seriously thought out, conceptually mature, personal moral or religious beliefs held to be fundamentally incompatible (that is, not merely inconsistent on the basis of selfish desires, whim or impulse), for example, either with all laws requiring [conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") for military service, or legal compulsion to fight for or financially support the State in a particular war.[^163] A famous example arose when [Henry David Thoreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_David_Thoreau "Henry David Thoreau") the author of *[Walden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden "Walden")* was willingly jailed for refusing to pay a tax because he profoundly disagreed with a government policy and was frustrated by the corruption and injustice of the democratic machinery of the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\) "State (polity)").[^164] A more recent case concerned [Kimberly Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberly_Rivera "Kimberly Rivera"), a private in the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army "US Army") and mother of four children who, having served three months in [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War") decided the conflict was immoral and sought refugee status in Canada in 2012 (see [List of Iraq War resisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iraq_War_resisters "List of Iraq War resisters")), but was deported and arrested in the US.[^165]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Benjamin_D._Maxham_-_Henry_David_Thoreau_-_Restored_-_greyscale_-_straightened.jpg/170px-Benjamin_D._Maxham_-_Henry_David_Thoreau_-_Restored_-_greyscale_-_straightened.jpg)

[Henry David Thoreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_David_Thoreau "Henry David Thoreau"): Must the citizen ever for a moment, or in the least degree, resign his conscience to the legislator?

> "Unjust laws exist; shall we be content to obey them, or shall we endeavour to amend them, and obey them until we have succeeded, or shall we transgress them at once? ... A man has not everything to do but something; and because he cannot do *everything*, it is not necessary that he should do *something* wrong ... It is for no particular item in the tax bill that I refuse to pay it. I simply wish to refuse allegiance to the State, to withdraw and stand aloof from it effectually. I do not care to trace the course of my dollar if I could, till it buys a man, or a musket to shoot one with—the dollar is innocent—but I am concerned to trace the effects of my allegiance ... Must the citizen ever for a moment, or in the least degree, resign his conscience to the legislator? Why has every man a conscience, then?"

— Henry David Thoreau. Civil Disobedience. 1848. reprinted Signet Classic, New York. 1960 pp. 228, 229, 236.

In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain") granted conscientious-objection status not just to complete [pacifists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist"), but to those who objected to fighting in that particular war; this was done partly out of genuine respect, but also to avoid the disgraceful and futile persecutions of [conscientious objectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objectors "Conscientious objectors") that occurred during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War").[^166]

[Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International") organises campaigns to protect those arrested and or incarcerated as a [prisoner of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience "Prisoner of conscience") because of their conscientious beliefs, particularly concerning intellectual, political and artistic freedom of expression and association.[^167] [Aung San Suu Kyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_San_Suu_Kyi "Aung San Suu Kyi") of Burma, was the winner of the 2009 [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International") [Ambassador of Conscience Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_of_Conscience_Award "Ambassador of Conscience Award"). In legislation, a [conscience clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_clause_\(medical\) "Conscience clause (medical)") is a provision in a statute that excuses a health professional from complying with the law (for example legalising surgical or pharmaceutical [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion "Abortion")) if it is incompatible with religious or conscientious beliefs.[^168] Expressed justifications for refusing to obey laws because of conscience vary. Many conscientious objectors are so for religious reasons—notably, members of the [historic peace churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_churches "Peace churches") are pacifist by doctrine. Other objections can stem from a deep sense of responsibility toward humanity as a whole, or from the conviction that even acceptance of work under military orders acknowledges the principle of [conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") that should be everywhere condemned before the world can ever become safe for real [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy").[^169] A conscientious objector, however, does not have a primary aim of changing the law.[^john_rawls_1971._pp._368%e2%80%9370-160] [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey "John Dewey") considered that conscientious objectors were often the victims of "moral innocency" and inexpertness in moral training: "the moving force of events is always too much for conscience".[^dykhuizen,_george_1978._p._165-170] The remedy was not to deplore the wickedness of those who manipulate world power, but to connect *conscience* with forces moving in another direction- to build institutions and social environments predicated on the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law"), for example, "then will conscience itself have compulsive power instead of being forever the martyred and the coerced."[^dykhuizen,_george_1978._p._165-170] As an example, [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") who had advocated *conscientious objection* during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") and had been a longterm supporter of [War Resisters' International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Resisters%27_International "War Resisters' International") reasoned that "radical pacifism" could not be justified in the face of [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") rearmament and advocated a world federalist organization with its own professional army.[^171] [Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Johnson "Samuel Johnson") pointed out that an appeal to conscience should not allow the law to bring unjust suffering upon another. *Conscience*, according to Johnson, was nothing more than a conviction felt by ourselves of something to be done or something to be avoided; in questions of simple unperplexed morality, *conscience* is very often a guide that may be trusted.[^life_of_johnson_505-172] But before *conscience* can conclusively determine what morally should be done, he thought that the state of the question should be thoroughly known.[^life_of_johnson_505-172] "No man's conscience", said Johnson "can tell him the right of another man ... it is a conscience very ill informed that violates the rights of one man, for the convenience of another."[^life_of_johnson_505-172]

[Civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience "Civil disobedience") as [nonviolent protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent_protest "Nonviolent protest") or [civil resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_resistance "Civil resistance") are also acts of conscience, but are designed by those who undertake them chiefly to change, by appealing to the majority and democratic processes, laws or government policies perceived to be incoherent with fundamental social virtues and principles (such as justice, equality or respect for intrinsic human dignity).[^173] Civil disobedience, in a properly functioning [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy"), allows a minority who feel strongly that a law infringes their sense of [justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice "Justice") (but have no capacity to obtain legislative amendments or a referendum on the issue) to make a potentially apathetic or uninformed majority take account of the intensity of opposing views.[^174] A notable example of civil resistance or [satyagraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyagraha "Satyagraha") ("satya" in [sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") means "truth and compassion", "agraha" means "firmness of will") involved [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi") making salt in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") when that act was prohibited by a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") statute, in order to create moral pressure for law reform.[^175] [Rosa Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Parks "Rosa Parks") similarly acted on conscience in 1955 in Montgomery, Alabama refusing a legal order to give up her seat to make room for a white passenger; her action (and the similar earlier act of 15-year-old [Claudette Colvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudette_Colvin "Claudette Colvin")) led to the [Montgomery bus boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_bus_boycott "Montgomery bus boycott").[^176] [Rachel Corrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Corrie "Rachel Corrie") was a US citizen allegedly killed by a bulldozer operated by the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces") (IDF) while involved in [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action "Direct action") (based on the nonviolent principles of [Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr. "Martin Luther King Jr.") and [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi")) to prevent demolition of the home of local [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people "Palestinian people") pharmacist Samir Nasrallah.[^177] [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore "Al Gore") has argued "If you're a young person looking at the future of this planet and looking at what is being done right now, and not done, I believe we have reached the stage where it is time for civil disobedience to prevent the construction of new coal plants that do not have carbon capture and sequestration."[^178] In 2011, NASA climate scientist [James E. Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Hansen "James E. Hansen"), environmental leader [Phil Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Radford "Phil Radford") and Professor [Bill McKibben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_McKibben "Bill McKibben") were arrested for opposing a tar sands oil pipeline[^179][^180] and Canadian renewable energy professor [Mark Jaccard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Jaccard "Mark Jaccard") was arrested for opposing mountain-top coal mining;[^181] in his book [Storms of my Grandchildren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storms_of_my_Grandchildren "Storms of my Grandchildren") Hansen calls for similar [civil resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_resistance "Civil resistance") on a global scale to help replace the 'business-as-usual' [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol "Kyoto Protocol") [cap and trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_and_trade "Cap and trade") system, with a progressive [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax "Carbon tax") at emission source on the oil, gas and coal industries – revenue being paid as dividends to low [carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint "Carbon footprint") families.[^182][^telltruth-183][^184]

Notable historical examples of *conscientious noncompliance* in a different professional context included the manipulation of the visa process in 1939 by Japanese Consul-General [Chiune Sugihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiune_Sugihara "Chiune Sugihara") in Kaunas (the temporary capital of [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania "Lithuania") between Germany and the Soviet Union) and by [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg "Raoul Wallenberg") in Hungary in 1944[^185] to allow Jews to escape almost certain death.[^186] [Ho Feng-Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Feng-Shan "Ho Feng-Shan") the Chinese Consul-General in Vienna in 1939, defied orders from the Chinese ambassador in Berlin to issue Jews with visas for Shanghai.[^187] [John Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rabe "John Rabe") a German member of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party") likewise saved thousands of Chinese from massacre by the Japanese military at [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre "Nanjing Massacre").[^188] The [White Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rose "White Rose") German student movement against the Nazis declared in their 4th leaflet: "We will not be silent. We are your bad conscience. The White Rose will not leave you in peace!"[^189] *Conscientious noncompliance* may be the only practical option for citizens wishing to affirm the existence of an international moral order or 'core' historical rights (such as the [right to life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_life "Right to life"), [right to a fair trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_a_fair_trial "Right to a fair trial") and [freedom of opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_opinion "Freedom of opinion")) in states where [non-violent protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-violent_protest "Non-violent protest") or [civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience "Civil disobedience") are met with prolonged [arbitrary detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary_detention "Arbitrary detention"), [torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture "Torture"), [forced disappearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance "Forced disappearance"), [murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder "Murder") or [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution "Persecution").[^190] The controversial [Milgram experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milgram_experiment "Milgram experiment") into [obedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obedience_\(human_behavior\) "Obedience (human behavior)") by [Stanley Milgram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Milgram "Stanley Milgram") showed that many people lack the [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological "Psychological") resources to openly resist [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority "Authority"), even when they are directed to act callously and inhumanely against an [innocent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocent "Innocent") victim.[^191]

World conscience is the [universalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universalism "Universalism") idea that with ready global communication, all people on [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") will no longer be morally estranged from one another, whether it be culturally, ethnically, or geographically; instead they will conceive ethics from the [utopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian "Utopian") point of view of the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe "Universe"), [eternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity "Eternity") or [infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity "Infinity"), rather than have their duties and obligations defined by forces arising solely within the restrictive boundaries of "blood and territory".[^autogenerated2001-5]

Often this derives from a [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") or [natural law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law") perspective, that for [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace") to be achieved, *conscience*, properly understood, should be generally considered as not necessarily linked (often destructively) to [fundamentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalist "Fundamentalist") religious ideologies, but as an aspect of [universal consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_consciousness "Universal consciousness"), access to which is the [common heritage of humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_humanity "Common heritage of humanity").[^192] Thinking predicated on the development of *world conscience* is common to members of the [Global Ecovillage Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Ecovillage_Network "Global Ecovillage Network") such as the [Findhorn Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findhorn_Foundation "Findhorn Foundation"), international conservation organisations like [Fauna and Flora International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_and_Flora_International "Fauna and Flora International"), as well as performers of [world music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_music "World music") such as [Alan Stivell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Stivell "Alan Stivell").[^193] [Non-government organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-government_organizations "Non-government organizations"), particularly through their work in agenda-setting, policy-making and implementation of human rights-related policy, have been referred to as the conscience of the world[^194]

[Edward O Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_O_Wilson "Edward O Wilson") has developed the idea of [consilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consilience "Consilience") to encourage coherence of global moral and scientific knowledge supporting the premise that "only unified learning, universally shared, makes accurate foresight and wise choice possible".[^195] Thus, *world conscience* is a concept that overlaps with the [Gaia hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia_hypothesis "Gaia hypothesis") in advocating a balance of moral, legal, scientific and economic solutions to modern transnational problems such as [global poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_poverty "Global poverty") and [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), through strategies such as [environmental ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_ethics "Environmental ethics"), [climate ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_ethics "Climate ethics"), [natural conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_\(ethic\) "Conservation (ethic)"), [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology"), [cosmopolitanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism "Cosmopolitanism"), [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") and [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development "Sustainable development"), [biosequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosequestration "Biosequestration") and legal protection of the [biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere "Biosphere") and [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity").[^196][^197][^198][^199][^200] The NGO [350.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/350.org "350.org"), for example, seeks to attract world conscience to the problems associated with elevation in atmospheric [greenhouse gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas "Greenhouse gas") concentrations.[^201][^202]

The [Green party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_party "Green party") politician [Bob Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Brown "Bob Brown") (who was arrested by the [Tasmanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmanian "Tasmanian") state police for a conscientious act of [civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience "Civil disobedience") during the [Franklin Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Dam "Franklin Dam") protest) expresses *world conscience* in these terms: "the universe, through us, is evolving towards experiencing, understanding and making choices about its future'; one example of policy outcomes from such thinking being a global tax (see [Tobin tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_tax "Tobin tax")) to alleviate global poverty and protect the biosphere, amounting to 1/10 of 1% placed on the worldwide speculative currency market.[^204] Such an approach sees *world conscience* best expressing itself through political reforms promoting democratically based [globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalisation "Globalisation") or *planetary democracy* (for example [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") voting for global governance organisations (see [world government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_government "World government")) based on the model of "one person, one vote, one value") which gradually will replace contemporary market-based globalisation.[^205]

The American cardiologist [Bernard Lown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lown "Bernard Lown") and the Russian cardiologist [Yevgeniy Chazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeniy_Chazov "Yevgeniy Chazov") were motivated in *conscience* through studying the catastrophic public health consequences of [nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_war "Nuclear war") in establishing [International Physicians for the Prevention of Nuclear War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Physicians_for_the_Prevention_of_Nuclear_War "International Physicians for the Prevention of Nuclear War") (IPPNW) which was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1985 and continues to work to "heal an ailing planet".[^206]  
Worldwide expressions of *conscience* contributed to the decision of the French government to halt atmospheric [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_tests "Nuclear tests") at [Mururoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mururoa "Mururoa") in the Pacific in 1974 after 41 such explosions (although below-ground nuclear tests continued there into the 1990s).[^207]

A challenge to *world conscience* was provided by an influential 1968 article by [Garrett Hardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_Hardin "Garrett Hardin") that critically analyzed the dilemma in which multiple individuals, acting independently after rationally consulting self-interest (and, he claimed, the apparently low 'survival-of-the-fittest' value of *conscience*\-led actions) ultimately destroy a shared limited resource, even though each acknowledges such an outcome is not in anyone's long-term interest.[^hardin68-148] Hardin's conclusion that commons areas are practicably achievable only in conditions of low population density (and so their continuance requires state restriction on the freedom to breed), created controversy additionally through his direct deprecation of the role of *conscience* in achieving individual decisions, policies and laws that facilitate global justice and peace, as well as [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") and [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development "Sustainable development") of world commons areas, for example including those officially designated such under [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") treaties (see [common heritage of humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_humanity "Common heritage of humanity")).[^208] Areas designated [common heritage of humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_humanity "Common heritage of humanity") under [international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_law "International law") include the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"), [Outer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Space "Outer Space"), deep [sea bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_bed "Sea bed"), [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), the world cultural and natural heritage (see [World Heritage Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Convention "World Heritage Convention")) and the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome "Human genome").[^209] It will be a significant challenge for *world conscience* that as world oil, coal, mineral, timber, agricultural and water reserves are depleted, there will be increasing pressure to commercially exploit [common heritage of mankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_mankind "Common heritage of mankind") areas.[^210]

The philosopher [Peter Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Singer "Peter Singer") has argued that the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals "Millennium Development Goals") represent the emergence of an ethics based not on national boundaries but on the idea of one world.[^211] [Ninian Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninian_Smart "Ninian Smart") has similarly predicted that the increase in global travel and communication will gradually draw the world's religions towards a pluralistic and transcendental humanism characterized by an "open spirit" of empathy and compassion.[^212]

[Garrett Hardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_Hardin "Garrett Hardin") doubted the capacity of *conscience* to protect such commons areas

[Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky "Noam Chomsky") has argued that forces opposing the development of such a world conscience include [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market "Free market") ideologies that valorise [corporate greed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_greed "Corporate greed") in nominal electoral [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracies "Democracies") where [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising "Advertising"), [shopping malls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_malls "Shopping malls") and indebtedness, shape [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens "Citizens") into [apathetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apathetic "Apathetic") [consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer "Consumer") in relation to information and access necessary for democratic participation.[^213] [John Passmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Passmore "John Passmore") has argued that mystical considerations about the global expansion of all human consciousness, should take into account that if as a species we do become something much superior to what we are now, it will be as a consequence of *conscience* not only implanting a goal of moral perfectibility, but assisting us to remain periodically anxious, passionate and discontented, for these are necessary components of care and compassion.[^214] The *Committee on Conscience* of the US Holocaust Memorial Museum has targeted [genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide") such as those in [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda"), [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia "Bosnia"), [Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur "Darfur"), the [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo") and [Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechnya "Chechnya") as challenges to the world's conscience.[^215] [Oscar Arias Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Arias_Sanchez "Oscar Arias Sanchez") has criticised global [arms industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_industry "Arms industry") spending as a failure of conscience by nation states: "When a country decides to invest in arms, rather than in education, housing, the environment, and health services for its people, it is depriving a whole generation of its right to prosperity and happiness. We have produced one firearm for every ten inhabitants of this planet, and yet we have not bothered to end hunger when such a feat is well within our reach. This is not a necessary or inevitable state of affairs. It is a deliberate choice" (see [Campaign Against Arms Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_Against_Arms_Trade "Campaign Against Arms Trade")).[^216] US House of Representatives Speaker [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi "Nancy Pelosi"), after meeting with the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama "14th Dalai Lama") during the [2008 violent protests in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tibetan_unrest "2008 Tibetan unrest") and aftermath said: "The situation in Tibet is a challenge to the conscience of the world."[^217] [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela "Nelson Mandela"), through his example and words, has been described as having shaped the conscience of the world.[^218]  
The [Right Livelihood Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Livelihood_Award "Right Livelihood Award") is awarded yearly in Sweden to those people, mostly strongly motivated by *conscience*, who have made exemplary practical contributions to resolving the great challenges facing our planet and its people.[^219] In 2009, for example, along with [Catherine Hamlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Hamlin "Catherine Hamlin") ([obstetric fistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_fistula "Obstetric fistula") and see [fistula foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fistula_foundation "Fistula foundation"))), [David Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Suzuki "David Suzuki") (promoting awareness of [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change")) and Alyn Ware ([nuclear disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_disarmament "Nuclear disarmament")), [René Ngongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Ngongo "René Ngongo") shared the [Right Livelihood Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Livelihood_Award "Right Livelihood Award") "for his courage in confronting the forces that are destroying the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin "Congo Basin")'s rainforests and building political support for their conservation and sustainable use".[^220][^221] [Avaaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avaaz "Avaaz") is one of the largest global on-line organizations launched in January 2007 to promote conscience-driven activism on issues such as [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights "Human rights"), [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights"), corruption, poverty, and conflict, thus "closing the gap between the world we have and the world most people everywhere want".[^222]

[Fyodor Dostoevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoevsky "Fyodor Dostoevsky"), author of *[Crime and Punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_and_Punishment "Crime and Punishment")*

The ancient epic of the Indian subcontinent, the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata") of [Vyasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyasa "Vyasa"), contains two pivotal moments of *conscience*. The first occurs when the warrior [Arjuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjuna "Arjuna") being overcome with compassion against killing his opposing relatives in war, receives counsel (see [Bhagavad-Gita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad-Gita "Bhagavad-Gita")) from [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna "Krishna") about his spiritual duty ("work as though you are performing a sacrifice for the general good").[^259] The second, at the end of the saga, is when king [Yudhishthira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yudhishthira "Yudhishthira") having alone survived the moral tests of life, is offered eternal bliss, only to refuse it because a faithful dog is prevented from coming with him by purported divine rules and laws.[^260] The French author [Montaigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montaigne "Montaigne") (1533–1592) in one of the most celebrated of [his essays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essays_\(Montaigne\) "Essays (Montaigne)") ("On experience") expressed the benefits of living with a clear conscience: "Our duty is to compose our character, not to compose books, to win not battles and provinces, but order and tranquillity in our conduct. Our great and glorious masterpiece is to live properly".[^261] In his famous Japanese travel journal *[Oku no Hosomichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oku_no_Hosomichi "Oku no Hosomichi")* (*Narrow Road to the Deep North*) composed of mixed [haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku "Haiku") poetry and prose, [Matsuo Bashō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsuo_Bash%C5%8D "Matsuo Bashō") (1644–94) in attempting to describe the eternal in this perishable world is often moved in *conscience*; for example by a thicket of summer grass being all that remains of the dreams and ambitions of ancient warriors.[^262] [Chaucer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaucer "Chaucer")'s "[Franklin's Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin%27s_Tale "Franklin's Tale")" in *[The Canterbury Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales "The Canterbury Tales")* recounts how a young suitor releases a wife from a [rash promise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rash_promise "Rash promise") because of the respect in his *conscience* for the freedom to be truthful, gentle and generous.[^263]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Eug%C3%A8ne_Delacroix_-_Hamlet_and_Horatio_in_the_Graveyard_-_WGA6199.jpg/170px-Eug%C3%A8ne_Delacroix_-_Hamlet_and_Horatio_in_the_Graveyard_-_WGA6199.jpg)

[Eugène Delacroix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Delacroix "Eugène Delacroix"), *[Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet "Hamlet") and Horatio in the Graveyard* (1839, oil on canvas)

The critic [A. C. Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._C._Bradley "A. C. Bradley") discusses the central problem of [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare")'s tragic character [Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet "Hamlet") as one where conscience in the form of moral scruples deters the young Prince with his "great anxiety to do right" from obeying his father's hell-bound ghost and murdering the usurping King ("is't not perfect conscience to quit him with this arm?" (v.ii.67)).[^264]

Bradley develops a theory about Hamlet's moral agony relating to a conflict between "traditional" and "critical" conscience: "The conventional moral ideas of his time, which he shared with the Ghost, told him plainly that he ought to avenge his father; but a deeper conscience in him, which was in advance of his time, contended with these explicit conventional ideas. It is because this deeper conscience remains below the surface that he fails to recognise it, and fancies he is hindered by [cowardice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowardice "Cowardice") or [sloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloth_\(deadly_sin\) "Sloth (deadly sin)") or [passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_\(emotion\) "Passion (emotion)") or what not; but it emerges into light in that speech to Horatio. And it is just because he has this nobler moral nature in him that we admire and love him".[^265] The opening words of Shakespeare's [Sonnet 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnet_94 "Sonnet 94") ("They that have pow'r to hurt, and will do none") have been admired as a description of *conscience*.[^266] So has [John Donne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Donne "John Donne")'s commencement of his poem *[s:Goodfriday, 1613. Riding Westward](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Goodfriday,_1613._Riding_Westward "s:Goodfriday, 1613. Riding Westward")*: "Let man's soul be a sphere, and then, in this, Th' intelligence that moves, devotion is;"[^267]

[Anton Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov "Anton Chekhov") in his plays *[The Seagull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seagull "The Seagull")*, *[Uncle Vanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Vanya "Uncle Vanya")* and *[Three Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Sisters_\(play\) "Three Sisters (play)")* describes the tortured emotional states of doctors who at some point in their careers have turned their back on conscience.[^268] In his short stories, [Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov "Anton Chekhov") also explored how people misunderstood the voice of a tortured conscience. A promiscuous student, for example, in *The Fit* describes it as a "dull pain, indefinite, vague; it was like anguish and the most acute fear and despair ... in his breast, under the heart" and the young doctor examining the misunderstood agony of compassion experienced by the factory owner's daughter in *From a Case Book* calls it an "unknown, mysterious power ... in fact close at hand and watching him."[^269] Characteristically, Chekhov's own conscience drove him on the long journey to [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin "Sakhalin") to record and alleviate the harsh conditions of the prisoners at that remote outpost. As Irina Ratushinskaya writes in the introduction to that work: "Abandoning everything, he travelled to the distant island of [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin "Sakhalin"), the most feared place of exile and forced labour in Russia at that time. One cannot help but wonder why? Simply, because the lot of the people there was a bitter one, because nobody really knew about the lives and deaths of the exiles, because he felt that they stood in greater need of help that anyone else. A strange reason, maybe, but not for a writer who was the epitome of all the best traditions of a Russian man of letters. Russian literature has always focused on questions of **conscience** and was, therefore, a powerful force in the moulding of public opinion."[^270]

[E. H. Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Carr "E. H. Carr") writes of [Dostoevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dostoevsky "Dostoevsky")'s character the young student Raskolnikov in the novel *[Crime and Punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_and_Punishment "Crime and Punishment")* who decides to murder a 'vile and loathsome' old woman money lender on the principle of transcending conventional morals: "the sequel reveals to us not the pangs of a stricken *conscience* (which a less subtle writer would have given us) but the tragic and fruitless struggle of a powerful intellect to maintain a conviction which is incompatible with the essential nature of man."[^271]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Hermann_Hesse_1927_Photo_Gret_Widmann.jpg/170px-Hermann_Hesse_1927_Photo_Gret_Widmann.jpg)

[Hermann Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Hesse "Hermann Hesse"), author of *[Siddhartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhartha_\(novel\) "Siddhartha (novel)")*

[Hermann Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Hesse "Hermann Hesse") wrote his *[Siddhartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhartha_\(novel\) "Siddhartha (novel)")* to describe how a young man in the time of the [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha "Buddha") follows his *conscience* on a journey to discover a transcendent inner space where all things could be unified and simply understood, ending up discovering that personal truth through selfless service as a ferryman.[^272] [J. R. R. Tolkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien "J. R. R. Tolkien") in his epic *[The Lord of the Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings")* describes how only the [hobbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobbit "Hobbit") [Frodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frodo "Frodo") is pure enough in *conscience* to carry the ring of power through war-torn [Middle-earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth "Middle-earth") to destruction in the [Cracks of Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracks_of_Doom "Cracks of Doom"), Frodo determining at the end to journey without weapons, and being saved from failure by his earlier decision to spare the life of the creature [Gollum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gollum "Gollum").[^273] [Conor Cruise O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Cruise_O%27Brien "Conor Cruise O'Brien") wrote that [Albert Camus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Camus "Albert Camus") was the writer most representative of the Western consciousness and conscience in its relation to the non-Western world.[^274] [Harper Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper_Lee "Harper Lee")'s 1960 novel *[To Kill a Mockingbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Kill_a_Mockingbird "To Kill a Mockingbird")* portrays [Atticus Finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atticus_Finch "Atticus Finch") (played by [Gregory Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Peck "Gregory Peck") in the classic film from the book (see *[To Kill a Mockingbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Kill_a_Mockingbird_\(film\) "To Kill a Mockingbird (film)")*)) as a lawyer true to his conscience who sets an example to his children and community.[^275]

The [Robert Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bolt "Robert Bolt") play *[A Man For All Seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Man_for_All_Seasons_\(play\) "A Man for All Seasons (play)")* focuses on the conscience of Catholic lawyer [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More "Thomas More") in his struggle with King [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII "Henry VIII") ("the loyal subject is more bounden to be loyal to his conscience than to any other thing").[^276] [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell") wrote his novel *[Nineteen Eighty-Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four "Nineteen Eighty-Four")* on the isolated island of [Jura, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura,_Scotland "Jura, Scotland") to describe how a man (Winston Smith) attempts to develop *critical conscience* in a totalitarian state which watches every action of the people and manipulates their thinking with a mixture of [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda "Propaganda"), endless war and thought control through language control (*double think* and *newspeak*) to the point where prisoners look up to and even love their torturers.[^277] In the Ministry of Love, Winston's torturer (O'Brien) states: "You are imagining that there is something called human nature which will be outraged by what we do and will turn against us. But we create human nature. Men are infinitely malleable".[^278]

A tapestry copy of [Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picasso "Picasso")'s *[Guernica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernica_\(painting\) "Guernica (painting)")* depicting a massacre of innocent women and children during the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") is displayed on the wall of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") building in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), at the entrance to the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council "Security Council") room, demonstrably as a spur to the conscience of representatives from the [nation states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_states "Nation states").[^279] [Albert Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Tucker_\(artist\) "Albert Tucker (artist)") painted *Man's Head* to capture the moral disintegration, and lack of conscience, of a man convicted of kicking a dog to death.[^280]

[Vincent van Gogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_van_Gogh "Vincent van Gogh"), 1890. [Kröller-Müller Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%B6ller-M%C3%BCller_Museum "Kröller-Müller Museum"). *[On the Threshold of Eternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_Eternity%27s_Gate "At Eternity's Gate")*.

The [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionist "Impressionist") painter [Vincent van Gogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_van_Gogh "Vincent van Gogh") wrote in a letter to his brother Theo in 1878 that "one must never let the fire in one's soul die, for the time will inevitably come when it will be needed. And he who chooses poverty for himself and loves it possesses a great treasure and will hear the voice of his **conscience** address him every more clearly. He who hears that voice, which is God's greatest gift, in his innermost being and follows it, finds in it a friend at last, and he is never alone! ... That is what all great men have acknowledged in their works, all those who have thought a little more deeply and searched and worked and loved a little more than the rest, who have plumbed the depths of the sea of life."[^281]

The 1957 [Ingmar Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman "Ingmar Bergman") film *[The Seventh Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seventh_Seal "The Seventh Seal")* portrays the journey of a [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval "Medieval") [knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight "Knight") (Max von Sydow) returning disillusioned from the [crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") ("what is going to happen to those of us who want to believe, but aren't able to?") across a [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\) "Plague (disease)")\-ridden landscape, undertaking a game of [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess") with the [personification of Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personifications_of_death "Personifications of death") until he can perform one meaningful altruistic act of conscience (overturning the chess board to distract Death long enough for a family of jugglers to escape in their wagon).[^282]  
The 1942 *[Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca_\(film\) "Casablanca (film)")* centers on the development of conscience in the cynical American Rick Blaine ([Humphrey Bogart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Bogart "Humphrey Bogart")) in the face of oppression by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis "Nazis") and the example of the resistance leader [Victor Laszlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Laszlo "Victor Laszlo").[^283]  
The [David Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lean "David Lean") and [Robert Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bolt "Robert Bolt") screenplay for *[Doctor Zhivago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Zhivago_\(film\) "Doctor Zhivago (film)")* (an adaptation of [Boris Pasternak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Pasternak "Boris Pasternak")'s novel) focuses strongly on the conscience of a doctor-poet in the midst of the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_\(1917\) "Russian Revolution (1917)") (in the end "the walls of his heart were like paper").[^284]  
The 1982 [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott") film *[Blade Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner "Blade Runner")* focuses on the struggles of conscience between and within a bounty hunter (Rick Deckard ([Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford "Harrison Ford"))) and a renegade [replicant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicant "Replicant") [android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(robot\) "Android (robot)") (Roy Batty ([Rutger Hauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutger_Hauer "Rutger Hauer"))) in a future society which refuses to accept that forms of artificial intelligence can have aspects of being such as conscience.[^285]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Bwv232-credo.jpg/170px-Bwv232-credo.jpg)

[J.S. Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach"). Original page from Credo (Symbolum Nicenum) section of [Mass in B minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_in_B_minor "Mass in B minor")

[Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach") wrote his last great choral composition the [Mass in B minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_in_B_minor "Mass in B minor") (BWV 232) to express the alternating emotions of loneliness, despair, joy and rapture that arise as *conscience* reflects on a departed human life.[^286] Here JS Bach's use of [counterpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterpoint "Counterpoint") and [contrapuntal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrapuntal "Contrapuntal") settings, his dynamic discourse of melodically and rhythmically distinct voices seeking forgiveness of sins ("*Qui tollis peccata mundi, miserere nobis*") evokes a spiraling moral conversation of all humanity expressing his belief that "with devotional music, God is always present in his grace".[^287]

[Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven "Ludwig van Beethoven")'s meditations on illness, conscience and mortality in the [Late String Quartets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_String_Quartets_\(Beethoven\) "Late String Quartets (Beethoven)") led to his dedicating the third movement of String Quartet in A Minor (1825) Op. 132 (see [String Quartet No. 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_No._15_\(Beethoven\) "String Quartet No. 15 (Beethoven)")) as a "Hymn of Thanksgiving to God of a convalescent".[^288][^289] [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon "John Lennon")'s work "[Imagine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_\(John_Lennon_song\) "Imagine (John Lennon song)")" owes much of its popular appeal to its evocation of conscience against the atrocities created by [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War "War"), [religious fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_fundamentalism "Religious fundamentalism") and [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics "Politics").[^290] [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison "George Harrison")\-written track "[The Inner Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Inner_Light_\(song\) "The Inner Light (song)")" sets to Indian [raga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga "Raga") music a verse from the *[Tao Te Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching "Tao Te Ching")* that "without going out of your door you can know the ways of heaven'.[^291] In the 1986 movie *[The Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mission_\(1986_film\) "The Mission (1986 film)")* the guilty conscience and penance of the slave trader Mendoza is made more poignant by the haunting oboe music of [Ennio Morricone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennio_Morricone "Ennio Morricone") ("On Earth as it is in Heaven")[^292] The song [Sweet Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Lullaby "Sweet Lullaby") by [Deep Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Forest "Deep Forest") is based on a traditional [Baegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baeggu_language "Baeggu language") [lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby "Lullaby") from the [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands "Solomon Islands") called "Rorogwela" in which a young orphan is comforted as an act of conscience by his older brother.[^293] The [Dream Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Academy "Dream Academy") song 'Forest Fire' provided an early warning of the moral dangers of our 'black cloud' 'bringing down a different kind of weather ... letting the sunshine in, that's how the end begins."[^294]

The [American Society of Journalists and Authors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Journalists_and_Authors "American Society of Journalists and Authors") (ASJA) presents the [Conscience-in-Media Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience-in-Media_Award "Conscience-in-Media Award") to [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalists "Journalists") whom the society deems worthy of recognition for demonstrating "singular commitment to the highest principles of journalism at notable personal cost or sacrifice".[^295]

The [Ambassador of Conscience Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_of_Conscience_Award "Ambassador of Conscience Award"), [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International")'s most prestigious [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights "Human rights") award, takes its inspiration from a poem written by Irish [Nobel prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_prize "Nobel prize")\-winning poet [Seamus Heaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamus_Heaney "Seamus Heaney") called "The Republic of Conscience".[^artforamnesty1-6]
