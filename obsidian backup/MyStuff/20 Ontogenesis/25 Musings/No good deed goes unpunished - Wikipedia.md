[Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas "Thomas Aquinas") contemplating deeds and punishment

The phrase "**No good deed goes unpunished**" is a [sardonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardonic "Sardonic") commentary on the frequency with which acts of kindness backfire on those who offer them. In other words, those who help others are doomed to suffer as a result of their helpfulness.

## Background\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=No_good_deed_goes_unpunished&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

The phrase is first attested in [Walter Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Map "Walter Map")'s 12th-century *[De nugis curialium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_nugis_curialium "De nugis curialium")*, in whose fourth chapter the character *Eudo* adhered to inverted morality "left no good deed unpunished, no bad one unrewarded".[\[1\]](#cite_note-:0-1)[\[2\]](#cite_note-2)

Conventional moral wisdom holds that evil deeds are punished by divine providence and good deeds are rewarded by divine providence:[\[1\]](#cite_note-:0-1)

> For as punishment is to the evil act, so is reward to a good act. Now no evil deed is unpunished, by God the just judge. Therefore no good deed is unrewarded, and so every good deed merits some good.[\[a\]](#cite_note-3)

This is related to the concepts of [Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell "Hell") and of [karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma "Karma").

### Modern expression\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=No_good_deed_goes_unpunished&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Modern expression")\]

The modern expression "No good deed goes unpunished" is an ironic twist on this conventional morality.[\[1\]](#cite_note-:0-1)

The ironic usage of the phrase appears to be\[*[weasel words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")*\] a 20th-century invention, found for example in [Brendan Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Gill "Brendan Gill")'s 1950 novel *The Trouble of One House*.[\[3\]](#cite_note-4) It is also featured prominently in the song "[No Good Deed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Good_Deed_(song) "No Good Deed (song)")", from the 2003 hit [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") musical *[Wicked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicked_(musical) "Wicked (musical)")*.[\[4\]](#cite_note-5) A satirical poem by [Franklin Pierce Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Pierce_Adams "Franklin Pierce Adams") with the title "*No Good Deed Goes Unpunished (So Shines a Good Deed in a Naughty World)*" also exists.[\[5\]](#cite_note-6)

In 2005, author [David Helvarg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Helvarg "David Helvarg") introduced the concept that the punishment may be a form of [retaliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retaliation "Retaliation"), in a piece he wrote for *[Grist Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grist_(magazine) "Grist (magazine)")*, "Remember that sign they hung up in an [EPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPA "EPA") office during the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ronald_Reagan "Presidency of Ronald Reagan"), 'No good deed goes unpunished'? Under [George Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush "Presidency of George W. Bush"), no good science goes unpunished."[\[6\]](#cite_note-7)
