### Homily: The Christian Theology of Hope

Good morning, brothers. As we gather here for this men's breakfast, let us reflect on the profound and transformative power of hope in Christian theology. Hope is not merely a passive wish for something better but a dynamic and living force that sustains and guides us. Today, I want to share with you three key aspects of Christian hope: its foundation in Christ, its sustaining power in our lives, and its ultimate fulfillment in eternity.

When people use the word *hope* they are expressing that the future, tomorrow, is going to be good, even better than today.  As Christians we can say, with all honesty that tomorrow is going to be better than today.  here is why...

#### 1. The Foundation of Hope: Christ our Anchor - The Resurrection

*Scripture:* 1 Peter 1:3 (NIV) - "Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! In his great mercy he has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead."

Our hope as Christians is rooted in the resurrection of Jesus Christ. This historical event is the cornerstone of our faith, demonstrating God's power over death and His promise of eternal life. Just as Christ was raised from the dead, we too can be confident in our future resurrection and eternal life with Him. This hope is not just wishful thinking but a living and active expectation that transforms our lives today.

Christian hope finds its foundation in the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. As it is written in Hebrews 6:19, "We have this hope as an anchor for the soul, firm and secure." Jesus, through His sacrifice, has given us the assurance of forgiveness and eternal life. This hope is not based on our own merits but on the unchanging and steadfast love of God. When we place our hope in Christ, we are anchored in a reality that transcends our circumstances and provides us with a firm foundation amidst the storms of life.

**Reflection:** Reflect on the times in your life when you have felt hopeless. How did turning to Christ provide you with the strength and assurance to face those challenges? Consider how you can share this hope with others who may be struggling.

Christian hope is not mere wishful thinking or blind optimism. It's a confident expectation rooted in the character of God and the promises He has made to us. The apostle Paul tells us that hope is the culmination of a process that begins with suffering and ends with the assurance of God's love.

In our lives as men, we face numerous challenges - in our families, careers, and personal lives. These trials can either break us or, through faith, become the very things that strengthen our hope. As we persevere through difficulties, our character is forged, and we develop a deeper trust in God's faithfulness.

**Theological point**:
Hope in Christian theology is closely tied to faith and love. It's not just about future expectations but about trusting God's presence and purpose in our current circumstances.

#### 2. The Sustaining Power of Hope: Living in Expectation

*Scripture:* Colossians 1:27
"To them God has chosen to make known among the Gentiles the glorious riches of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory."

Hope is a sustaining power that enables us to live with expectation and purpose. In Romans 8:24-25, Paul reminds us, "For in this hope we were saved. But hope that is seen is no hope at all. Who hopes for what they already have? But if we hope for what we do not yet have, we wait for it patiently." Hope gives us the strength to endure hardships, to persevere in faith, and to remain steadfast in our commitment to God. It inspires us to live out our calling, knowing that our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed in us.

**Reflection:** Think about the challenges you are currently facing. How does the hope of God's promises help you to persevere and remain faithful? How can you encourage your brothers in Christ to hold on to this sustaining hope?  

As Christian men, where do we place our hope? In our abilities? Our achievements? Our possessions? Paul reminds us that our ultimate hope is in Christ Himself. The phrase "Christ in you, the hope of glory" encapsulates a profound truth: our hope is not just about what Christ has done for us, but about His ongoing presence in our lives.

This hope transforms how we view our daily lives. Whether we're at work, with our families, or facing personal struggles, we carry within us the power and presence of Christ. This indwelling presence is our assurance of future glory and our strength for today.

**Theological point**:
The doctrine of Christ's indwelling presence is central to Christian hope. It connects our present reality with our future expectation, giving us both comfort and motivation in our daily walk.


#### 3. The Fulfillment of Hope: Eternal Glory

*Scripture:* 1 Peter 3:15
"But in your hearts revere Christ as Lord. Always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect."

The ultimate fulfillment of our hope is the eternal glory that awaits us in the presence of God. Revelation 21:4 paints a beautiful picture of this future: "He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the old order of things has passed away." This hope gives us a future-oriented perspective, reminding us that our current trials are temporary and that there is a glorious inheritance awaiting us. It motivates us to live with eternity in mind, investing in what truly matters and seeking to advance God's kingdom here on earth.

**Reflection:** Consider how the hope of eternal life shapes your daily decisions and priorities. Are there areas where you need to realign your focus to reflect this eternal perspective? How can you encourage others to live with this hope in their hearts?
Hope is not meant to be a private consolation but a public testimony. Peter challenges us to be ready to explain our hope to others. This requires us to cultivate two things: a deep reverence for Christ in our hearts, and a gentle, respectful attitude towards others.

As men, we often feel pressure to have all the answers or to appear strong at all times. But true strength comes from acknowledging our dependence on Christ and humbly sharing the source of our hope with others. Our hope should be evident in how we face challenges, treat others, and prioritize our lives.

**Theological point**:
The eschatological (end times) aspect of Christian hope doesn't just point us to a future reality; it shapes our present actions and attitudes, making us witnesses to the transforming power of the Gospel.

### Conclusion

Brothers, as we conclude our time together this morning, let us hold fast to the hope we have in Christ. May it be an anchor for our souls, a sustaining power in our lives, and a vision of the eternal glory that awaits us. Let us encourage one another with these truths and go forth, living as men of hope, ready to share the reason for our hope with a world in desperate need of it.

Let us pray.
