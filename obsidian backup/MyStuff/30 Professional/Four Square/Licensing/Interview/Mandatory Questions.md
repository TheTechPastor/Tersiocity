# What are two ways that The Foursquare Church has a modified the Episcopal form of government?
- **1. Centralized Authority vs. Shared Governance**

• **Foursquare**: Authority is more centralized in the International Church of the Foursquare Gospel, especially in its founding and leadership structure. Key decisions are often made by a board of directors or denominational leadership rather than through wide representation.

• **Episcopal Church**: While it has bishops, it practices _shared governance_—decisions are made through **General Convention**, involving both clergy and lay delegates voting together.
⸻
**2. Appointment of Leaders vs. Election of Bishops**
• **Foursquare**: Leaders, including pastors and overseers, are typically **appointed** by denominational leadership. This top-down structure ensures doctrinal consistency and accountability.
• **Episcopal Church**: Bishops and many church leaders are **elected** by diocesan conventions made up of clergy and lay members, reflecting a more democratic and participatory model.
—
- Good answers! I would adjust to your first answer to include that Foursquare modifies the episcopal form by incorporating 2 additional polity models, namely 1) Presbyterian (decisions made by committees) and 2) congregational (decisions made by the collective membership). Sort of a best-of-all models approach
