Have you ever noticed there’s a difference between planning and preparation? 🤔  
  
I didn’t consider this in-depth until I found an old paper planner. It contained many plans and goals I failed to achieve. Some I’m still failing at, (quit sugar? Never heard of it), and others I have found a way to accomplish or at least work regularly towards. When reflecting on what went wrong with my goals of yesteryear, it dawned on me… 

###   
Planning is NOT preparation

  

While planning and preparation may seem similar, they play distinct roles in setting you up for success. It’s easiest to show what I mean by listing some examples:  
  
🏋️Going to the gym:

-   Planning is putting a gym session or class on your calendar.
    
-   Preparation looks like laying out your clothes, filling your water bottle, and grabbing a towel beforehand.
    
      
    

👩‍💻Working on a project:

-   Planning is setting aside an afternoon to focus on a work project.
    
-   Preparation involves minimizing interruptions in advance and ensuring you have all the information you need to get started.
    
      
    

🍫Eating less sugar:

-   Planning is deciding to cut down on sugar intake for the next 30 days.
    
-   Preparation means ridding your pantry of temptation and finding healthy alternatives for when cravings (inevitably) strike.
    
      
    

🎨Spending quality time with your children:

-   Planning is making a conscious decision to be more present with your kids.
    
-   Preparation involves setting intentional boundaries around work and chores to ring-fence time to be with them. You could also search for “fun activities kids enjoy” and hope none involve glitter, paint, or permanent markers.
    
      
    

As Tom Foster puts it in his book [Hiring Talent](https://todoist.us18.list-manage.com/track/click?u=add87d5d9e06cf034200efe61&id=3b08842ea7&e=54d111ca1c):

**"Planning is the intention of doing something; preparation is the intention of succeeding at that."**

The key difference lies in knowing what you want to do (the plan) and giving yourself the best chance of following through with that plan (the preparation). 

  

### Real-life examples

  

Here are some personal experiences where planning without proper preparation led me to failure:

📽️ **Turning Todoist blog content into YouTube videos:** This plan has been in place for a while, but lack of preparation (dedicating time to camera/mic setup, preparing notes, etc.) means this has become that daunting task that gets batted into next week. Like an eternal game of “not it.” 

🧺 **Decluttering my home:** The intention to declutter was there. In fact, the decluttering happened, if you count deciding I don’t want stuff anymore and leaving it lying in organized piles around my house. However, without preparation like bags or boxes ready to sort items and a proper exit strategy, my house is still scattered with many a random stack of stuff.

  

### How to prepare

  

Since coming to this realization, I’ve started seeing better results in my goals. I plan for the week/month ahead, but then I schedule time in my calendar that I call “Prep time” before any tasks I want to accomplish. It may be 30 mins before a meeting or 10 mins the night before I want to make that hot yoga class at 9 am.

The other interesting side effect of giving myself dedicated time for preparation is that I feel much less resistance toward the task. Preparation feels like a bit of healthy procrastination, offering low stakes activity towards a task. Then, when I come to execute the plan, it feels frictionless.

So consider embracing both planning and preparation as distinct and separate parts of your productivity process. Allow time for both. The alternative is that, like me, your best-laid plans can end up in a dust-covered planner on a “decluttered” pile of stuff that lives in your hallway. 😬

## **✅ Todoist quick tip**

Prepare every little detail of your tasks in [Task View](https://todoist.us18.list-manage.com/track/click?u=add87d5d9e06cf034200efe61&id=09a50e7f1d&e=54d111ca1c). Include comments, sub-tasks, links, and attachments alongside all your usual options like due date and priority. Each task can house all the preparation you need to make completing it frictionless and oh-so-satisfying. 💆
