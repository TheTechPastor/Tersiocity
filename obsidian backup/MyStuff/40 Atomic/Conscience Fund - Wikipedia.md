# [Conscience Fund - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The **Conscience Fund** is one of three gift funds maintained by the [United States Department of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury") and is used for voluntary contributions from people who have stolen from or defrauded the [United States Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Government "United States Government"). [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Conscience_Fund-1) The fund was created in 1811 and received US$5 during its first year and over US$5.7million during its first 175 years. [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Time-2) The fund's name comes from a letter sent during the Civil War from a former [Quartermaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartermaster "Quartermaster") in the Army to then-[Treasurer of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_United_States "Treasurer of the United States") [Francis E. Spinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_E._Spinner "Francis E. Spinner") with a US$1500 check for previously misappropriated funds saying, "Suppose we call this a contribution to the conscience fund and get it announced in the newspapers, and perhaps we will get some more". [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-NYT-3)

Donations given to the Conscience Fund vary in size and reason. A 9 [cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cent_\(United_States_coin\) "Cent (United States coin)") donation was made by a person from [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") who had reused a 3 cent [postage stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamp "Postage stamp"), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-NYT-3) while a person from [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City "Jersey City") sent US$40,000 in several installments for US$8,000 he had previously taken. [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-4) Another donor sent handmade quilts in an effort to settle her tax bill. [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-5) Most gifts to the Conscience Fund are from anonymous donors. [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Conscience_Fund-1) Others are forwarded by clergy who have received [deathbed confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathbed_conversion "Deathbed conversion"). [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-NYT-3) The sincerity of some donors' repentance can be uncertain, as demonstrated by a received letter reading, "Dear Internal Revenue Service, I have not been able to sleep at night because I cheated on last year's income tax. Enclosed find a cashier's check for $1,000. If I still can't sleep, I'll send you the balance." [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Gazette-6)

Due to the fund's association with ill-gotten monies, some people object to having the funds they return associated with the fund. An example of this is when [then-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POTUS "POTUS") [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover") and his [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet "United States Cabinet") volunteered to reduce their pay in 1932 and instructed the Treasury to place the returned salary in the General Fund instead of the Conscience Fund. [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Hoover-7)

Donations to the Conscience Fund are not [tax deductible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_deductible "Tax deductible"), although gifts to the U.S. Treasury through the U.S. Fund are. [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_Fund?wprov=sfti1#cite_note-Gazette-6)
