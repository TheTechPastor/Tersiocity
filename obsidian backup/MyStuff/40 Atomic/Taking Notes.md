## **Why bother taking notes**

  

Even though it can feel tedious, there are several benefits to note-taking that go beyond just storing stuff:

1. **Active thinking / comprehension**
    
    When you write something down (or type it, or sketch it), you are forced to decide what matters. That filtering helps you understand and remember more than simply reading or listening. 
    
2. **Retention & memory**
    
    Summarizing in your own words, making notes, helps your brain encode the information better. If you don’t write things down, chances are you’ll forget key bits, especially details or connections. 
    
3. **Reference / mental offloading**
    
    Notes let you offload from memory those details you don’t need all the time, but want to have access to later. Especially helpful for ideas, quotes, facts, plans, etc. So you don’t have to keep everything in your head. 
    
4. **Focus & engagement**
    
    Taking notes can help you pay more attention, keep you engaged (in meetings, lectures, podcasts, etc.). If you’re mentally passive, you might drift or miss things. 
    
5. **Creativity & connections**
    
    When you collect a lot of notes over time, patterns emerge. You see connections between ideas that otherwise aren’t obvious. That can help with problem solving, planning, etc. Also gives you raw material for future projects. 
    
6. **Planning / tracking / actions**
    
    Notes aren’t just for “ideas.” They can help you plan, track what needs follow-up, keep to­dos / reminders. If done well, note-taking becomes part journaling, part planning. 
    
7. **Review & learning**
    
    Notes make review easier — whether for studying, refreshing memory before a meeting, or building something. Having your own summary helps more than going back to original raw material all over again.

## **Why you might not feel like doing it

  

Because knowing _why_ doesn’t always make it easier. Some common reasons people avoid note-taking:

- It feels like “just another thing” in overload.
    
- If you don’t know _what_ kind of notes are useful, it feels like busy work.
    
- Notes often accumulate without ever being reviewed, so they feel wasted.
    
- Switching between apps / too much friction: logging in, opening the app, deciding where to put it, how to structure it.
    
- Perfectionism or overwhelm (“I need good structure, the perfect app, it has to be well organized”) — and that stops you from doing anything.
    

---

## **How to make note-taking easier / more likely to stick**

  

Here are some strategies:

- **Start very small.** Maybe just one note per day, or one bullet idea whenever you have it.
    
- **Set a purpose**: Know what you _want_ notes for. Is it to remember books, track ideas, plan projects, meetings, etc.? If you know that, you can design how you take notes.
    
- **Review / integrate** weekly or so. Go back to your notes, highlight what’s still applicable, maybe extract actions. If notes just sit, they lose value.
    
- **Make it easy:** minimize friction. Using tools/app you already have, or tools that open quickly. Dictate voice notes if that’s faster. Use templates or systems that reduce decision fatigue.
    
- **Style matters**: Some people like free-form, others like structure. Some like command-line or plain text, others rich formatting. If it feels enjoyable or “flowy”, you’re more likely to do it.
